200  The Great Wood  Stonetop sits on a bluff overlooking the Great Wood, which stretches hundreds of miles to the east. Few villagers dare to even cross the   Stream   (page 396) that flows near the base of the bluff. Hunters, trappers, and foragers go deeper, but even they venture a day or two into the forest at most.  Impressions Always  Massive trunks, reaching up up up,  some bigger around than houses  Pale sky glimpsed through the canopy  Trees bending, creaking, rustling in  the wind  A skittering in the distance  A sense of smallness, of being swallowed  up by something ancient and vast  Spring  Feet slipping on mud, rotting leaves  Drip drip dripping of snowmelt,  rain, mist  Pale buds and early blooms, blue and  yellow and violet  Hopeful swell of birdsong  Summer  Brilliant, overwhelming green  Drone of insects buzzing, birds chirping  Oppressive heat and humidity  Autumn  A world aflame with reds and golds  Crunch of needles and leaves underfoot  Stark long shadows cast by a low sun  Sharp smell of cold, damp air  Winter  A frosty coating, a deathly stillness  Shocking white of snow drifts  Trees like boney fingers  Surprising chirp and rustle of finches The terrain is hilly and rugged, laced with small streams and dotted with ponds. The trees are a mix of deciduous and evergreen. In some spots, the trees grow tightly, surrounded by thick brush so that one can barely walk. In others, massive old growth trees dominate, the space between them sparse and open.
