It flows down from the   Whitefang Mountains   (page 480), through the   North and South Manmarch  (pages 282 and 350), fed by numerous creeks and streams along the way.  The Dread River  Impressions Always  From afar: a sense of stillness  As you get closer: the rush of the current  The far shore: ruin-dotted, lonely, mysterious  Near the water: an uneasy feeling, like anything could be in there— watching, waiting, hungry  Spring  The river swollen and flooded from snowmelt, spilling out over its banks  Swarms of flies, like clouds of smoke  The smell of mud and rotting fish  Summer  Brilliant green along the shores, sunlight sparkling on water  Endless chorus of waterfowl, starlings, insects, current  The occasional splash—just a fish, or a spooked turtle, maybe?  Autumn  Riot of autumn colors along the shores, reflected in the water  Blankets of mist, hiding the river but amplifying its rush and gurgle  Honk and flap of waterfowl flying south, or wheeling overhead before alighting  Winter  White-capped trees and bluffs and slopes  Ice growing out from the shores  Slate grey water, more menacing than ever with the promise of deadly cold In its lower reach, the river itself is wide and deep, never less than a quarter-mile across and dozens—if not hundreds—of feet deep. From afar, it appears placid and still. But it flows with a terrible and relentless current. The western shore alternates between wet- lands, wide-open flood plains, and rugged bluffs, ruins scattered all along its length. It would be an idyllic place to live, except that the length of the river is cursed.
