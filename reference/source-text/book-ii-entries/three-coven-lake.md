438  Three Coven Lake  Deep in the   Steplands   (page 372) lies a large lake, eight miles long by three. The waters are murky, often choppy, shallow in some places, in others 100 feet deep. Rocky islands poke from the water. Bluffs line much of the shore. But look closer. Those islands are the feet of ancient towers, and those bluffs are riddled with rock- cut rooms. For long ago, in the days of the   Stone Lords   (page 382), this was a mighty city. Teirstered, they called it. The City of Three Stars, rivalled only by great  Dulenn   (page 48). Picture it! See it in your mind! The stone and crystal spires, rising from the lake. Their glowing tips of   makerglass   (page 388), shining in the night. The walkways, stretched between the towers, so that one might walk the valley’s length without ever setting foot on shore. Those walkways were more than just convenience. They formed a mighty rune, a seal that helped to bind the power of the  Things Below   (page 412). The ceaseless march of countless feet powered the rune, like how water turns a wheel. When the Stone Lords’ servants rebelled, they sank the city of Dulenn in one night. But here, in Teirstered, things took longer. Mobs rose up against their masters and besieged them in their towers. They broke the walkways, called up horrors from the deep. Towers swayed, then crumbled, then fell beneath the waves. Soon, the city lay in ruins. But it was not abandoned. The   Barrow Builders   (page 34) fought over its bones. They sought the treasures of their fallen masters, and to seat themselves upon their thrones. Their armies spilled each others’ blood. The waters drank it up. In time, there was little left to fight over. In time, the Things Below withdrew. The tyrants of the Barrow Builders were cast down, or imprisoned, or left alone to rot. Teirstered, the City of Three Stars, was no more. But the blood-drunk waters of Three Coven Lake remained.
