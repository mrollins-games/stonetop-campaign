314  Half a day east of Stonetop is a copse of trees that turn blood red (leaves, bark, and all) at the autumnal equinox. By spring, the color has returned to normal. The   Forest Folk   (page 150) warned the villagers to avoid this place. They never said why.  An iron tang in the air  The scrutiny of the forest upon you, heavy like a blanket  Words of warning, faintly heard in the breeze, in the rustling leaves, in the insect hum and birdsong.  Spring/Summer:   bright green shoots/ leaves bursting from black bark and mud  Autumn/Winter:   overwhelming, glossy red  Tip:   faintly whisper things like “turn back” or “not welcome” or “go away” and then pretend you didn’t say anything  Lore  Something interesting:   A dozen such groves dot the Great Wood. Some redden in the fall, others in spring. Some, like the one near Stonetop, are large and old; others are just a few young trees.  Something useful:   When you   enter a red grove , you draw the forest’s wrath unless… …   you come naked and empty handed,  having been ritually purified; …   you bear a dying spirit, wishing to pass  on; or …   you are cloaked in subtle magic. Redwood serves as a conduit for   spirits of the wild   (page 356). Some of the Forest Folks’ most powerful totems are made of redwood. Alas, harvesting redwood with- out the permission of the forest spirits is sure to draw their primal, relentless ire.  Why are they so special?  That’s for you to decide. Maybe... …   they are where   spirits of the wild  (page 356) are born and where they come to die; …   they each imprison some terrible evil; …   they each mark the grave of a cham-  pion of   Danu   (page 64); or …   it’s different for each grove.
