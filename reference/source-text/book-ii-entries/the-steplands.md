372  The Steplands are a rugged hill-country south of the   Flats   (page 126). To the north, they touch   Ferrier’s Fen   (page 116) and the   Great Wood   (page 200). To the east, they give way to the   South Manmarch   (page 350). To the west, they climb higher and higher, like a stairway to the   Huffel Peaks   (page 236). The region is a karst—a plateau of lime- stone and quartzite and granite, carved away by ages of runoff and dissolved by groundwater, leaving a jumble of hills and valleys and rocky cliffs, all shot through with caves, sinkholes, and streams. The land seems wild and untamed to out- siders, but in truth it is home to dozens of small nomadic bands, the   Hillfolk  (page 228). 373  A rugged land  By all rights, the Steplands should be lush and fertile, teeming with farms and pas- tures. The same storms that soak the Flats roll through these hills, and snowmelt from the Huffel Peaks feeds many streams and aquifers. And yet: the region is rocky, almost barren in places. The slopes boast meadows of sedge and shortgrass and thickets of skinny pine, but the soil is thin and prone to erosion and mudslides. Cliffs and outcrops abound, dotted with shrubs and gnarled trees and even succulents normally found only in the south. There’s life a-plenty, sure—enough to support the roaming bands of Hillfolk and their herds—but one would be hard-pressed to make a go of farming. Why is the land like this? That’s for you and your table to decide, but perhaps…  …   it’s a natural result of the thirsty bedrock, the ceaseless southerly wind, the grazing of beasts; …   the   Stone Lords ’ (page 382) magic still infuses the land, repelling soil and seed, fossilizing roots, devouring minerals; …   battles between Stone Lords and the  Barrow Builders   (page 34) blasted the land, and only the hardiest   spirits of the wild   (page 356) still dwell here; …   the   Things Below   (page 412) sleep fitfully here, breathing in through lakes and sinkholes, sucking at the lifeforce of the soil; …   some combination of the above; or …   something completely different.
