But the land is dotted with burnt-out home- steads and ruined forts: a mute testament to those who have tried to settle here before. Tried, and failed. 351  South Manmarch South Manmarch  A long-disputed land  Before the   Cataclysm   (page 412) the Dread River formed the eastern border of   Stone Lord   (page 382) territory. After the Stone Lords fell, the   Barrow Builders   (page 34) fought each other back and forth across this expanse, grinding themselves down, like shards of shale abraded to dust over time. Now, the South Manmarch is the border between the civilized south and the hin- terlands of the World’s End. It’s a lawless place, full of dangerous beasts and even more dangerous people. Nomadic Hillfolk claim much of the region, and   bandits  (page 355) are a constant threat. And yet, the lure of the prairie is great. Every year or so, someone tries to carve out a piece of for themselves. A new settlement might be:  A family from Marshedge or the North Manmarch, starting their own farm  An ambitious Lygosi merchant, investing in a new trading post or caravansary  Some would-be chief or warlord from the   Manmarchers   (page 282), build- ing a new hamlet or hillfort  The   conquering armies   of the South (page 262), establishing a foothold Such settlements rarely last. They fall prey to natural disaster or roving beasts, to rivals or bandits, or to the certain wrath of the Hillfolk who claim these lands as theirs. Or perhaps, if you believe the stories, there’s something darker afoot. Perhaps these lands really are haunted. Haunted by the numerous souls who’ve died here. Or by foul things, slithering out of the Dread River. Perhaps the land is cursed, and no settlement can ever survive. Perhaps.  The Flintwash  Draining out of the Steplands and into the Dread River, this watercourse draws thickets, copses of cottonwood, wildlife, and wanderers to its gentle banks. The Hillfolk call it   Maengwalc   in their own tongue—“Flintwash,” after the stony plateau from which its headwaters spring. Hundreds of rills and brooks feed the Flintwash, and in its lower reaches, it’s quite broad and difficult to cross. Yet ways do exist. How might a traveler get from one side to the other? Ask a PC who would know, but perhaps there is… …   an old bridge from the time of the   Makers   (page 264); …   a newer bridge, built at great expense by some southern merchant or noble; …   a shallow ford; wide, rocky and usually safe; …   a ferry, run by enterprising souls; …   some combination of the above; or …   something else entirely.
