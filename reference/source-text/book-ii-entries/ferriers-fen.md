120  The Willow Witches  Many tales are told of the weird sisters of the Fen. There are always three, but they might be old, young, or middling aged. They might be tall and regal or twisted and gnarled, dignified and poised or feral things rooting through the mud. Sometimes, the tale’s hero seeks them out. Other times, they stumble upon the Witch’s abode. All stories agree that the Willow Witches have what you seek. An answer. An artifact (or knowledge of where to find it). A way to accomplish some impossible deed. Ev- eryone knows that Sianna Ferrier learned the secrets of the Fen from the Witches, allowing Marshedge to be settled. What the tales often fail to mention is: there’s always a price. For Sianna Ferrier, the Witches’ gifts were costly indeed: a child of her blood, or the blood of those she settled with, to be delivered every seven years. The witches are, in truth, a single entity, the inscrutable   incarnation   (page 300) of a primordial being of knowledge, fate, costs and consequences, greatly diminished and dwelling in this broken land.  The Witches’ abode  The Willow Witches’ abode is rarely found in the same place twice, and in every tale, the details are different. Sometimes they dwell in the hollow of a willow tree, the space inside impossibly big. In other tales, it’s a crude shack leaning against a tree. In still others, they huddle around a cookfire in a willow’s shade, no real shelter to speak of. The surest way to find the Witches is to seek out a   fen-troll   (page 123) and con- vince them to guide you. They all know the way, though one should expect mischief.  Mind your manners  The Willow Witches observe the old ways of hospitality. Any who approach peaceably and invoke the guest-right are welcome to eat their food and shelter under their roof (such that it is). The Witches will do no true harm unless the guest first violates the rules of hospitality by… …   initiating violence; …   threatening the Witches or their guests; …   stealing; or …   insulting the Witches’ hospitality. The Witches delight in provoking guests: speaking in riddles, asking about shame- ful secrets, offering disgusting victuals or shabby accommodations, showing glimpses of horrors that are gone when one looks again. The humble, patient, and wise do well. Hot-headed, prideful fools do not.  The price  Whatever one asks of the Willow Witches, they require something of similar value. Tailor the price to the character. It’s not about what the Willow Witches want or what they’ll do with it. It’s about sacrifice. It’s about what the seeker is asking for, and what it’s worth to them. The Witches might ask for… …   a promise, to be fulfilled now or later . Renege, and calamity follows. “The axiom of local contagion? Such fancy words for so simple a thing. We can teach you, yes, but you must swear to never again open a door without its owner’s permission.”  Ferrier’s Fen Ferrier’s Fen
