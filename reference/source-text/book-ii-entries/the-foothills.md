144  The Foothills  Two days northwest of   Stonetop, the rolling  Flats   (page 126) give way to hills, climbing up into the   Whitefang Mountains   (page 480). The   Highway   (page 270) cuts through these hills, leading to   Barrier Pass   (page 26). To the east, the hills wrap around the  Great Wood   (page 200). The Foothills are rugged and forest-strewn, crisscrossed with streams, dotted with gorges and ponds and small lakes. In ages past, it was a borderland between the   Rime Lords   (page 316) and the   Green Lords  (page 210), and later it was the extent of the   Barrow Builders’   reach (page 34). Nowadays, it is mostly uninhabited, but both Stonetop and Barrier Pass come here to harvest timber.  Impressions Always  Hills rising up from the Flats, mountains looming behind them  Young thin trees on the southernmost slopes, getting bigger and older as you press into the hills  Echoes of birdsong, beast-bellows, tumbling rocks, raised voices  Spring  Thin branches drooping with fresh green leaves  Mud slipping and squishing underfoot  Surprising chill of shadowed vales, where snow still lingers  Summer  Muggy, humid heat  Sweat stinging your eyes as you hump up another slope  Cold clear water from a gurgling stream, tasting better than anything  Autumn  Slopes turned red and orange and yellow, then black and brown and gray  Plump squirrels and other critters, busily foraging for winter  Cold, clammy mist lasting well into the day  Winter  Waist-deep snow drifts in the valleys  Frozen-over streams, water gurgling ominously from below  Squeak of trees, swaying in the cold wind  Short, magical twilight as the sun drops below the mountains

146  The Foothills The Foothills  Logging trips  A typical logging trip generates a wagon- load of timber (Value 2), and requires:  A wagon and two horses;  ~20 able-bodied workers; and  Pulling Together over 9-12 days (for travel and work) Trips traditionally take place in autumn, after the harvest is complete and only if there are no more-pressing needs in town. The   Palisade   steading improvement requires ~1,000 logs of good timber (Value 3). A wagonload of timber (Value 2) contains ~40-50 logs, meaning you need ~20-25 wagonloads. To gather that much timber:  You need ~20 able-bodied workers, a wagon, and two horses working for the course of a season  You must talk to a few town elders, and get their support  You risk accidents and attacks  You must spend 1 Surplus (to feed and equip the loggers) and reduce Fortunes by 1 (because of so many people and resources being away for so long)  The steading must Pull Together over the course of the season
