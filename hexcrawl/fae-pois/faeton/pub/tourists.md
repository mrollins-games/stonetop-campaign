# The Tourists

**Location:** The Pub, Faeton  
**Type:** Ard Sidhe (group of 4)  
**Role:** Social encounter / worldbuilding / mild antagonists

## Description

Four tall men seated together at a table, unhurried and faintly amused by everything. They are **Ard Sidhe** — the Tall Ones, high-born Fae of ancient lineage from **Ard Dún**, far deeper in. They are not from this forest — they crossed through other territories to get here.

### Appearance
- Unusually tall and lean, with long limbs and precise posture
- Sharp, angular features — high cheekbones, narrow jaws, prominent brows
- Pointed ears, partially hidden by long, straight hair (shades of silver, dark brown, pale gold)
- Skin smooth and ageless — could be thirty, could be three hundred
- Eyes bright and watchful, with an unsettling steadiness — they blink less than mortals
- Dressed in fine but understated travelling clothes — layered tunics, soft leather, muted greens and greys
- Each carries something small and well-made: a pipe, a slim journal, a carved cup, a folding knife

### Demeanor
- Speak softly among themselves in a language PCs don't recognize
- Polite if approached — even warm — but clearly *visiting*, not local
- Watch the room the way scholars watch an experiment
- Find mortal behavior gently entertaining, never mocking
- Occasionally one will sketch something in the journal or gesture toward a PC with raised eyebrows, as if noting something interesting

## What They're Actually Doing Here

They're slumming. Back in Ard Dún they're unremarkable — mid-status, unexceptional among their own kind. Out here in the shallow territories they're *fascinating*. Tall, beautiful, wealthy by local standards, from somewhere impressive-sounding. They enjoy:

- **The attention.** Locals treat them like visiting dignitaries. They aren't.
- **The simplicity.** Faeton is quaint and uncomplicated compared to home. They find it "refreshing" and "authentic."
- **The admiration.** They drop Ard Dún into conversation casually, watching it land. They like being asked about home.
- **The company.** They flirt broadly and without commitment. Charming, attentive, gone by morning. They've done this tour before — Faeton isn't their first stop and won't be their last.
- **The cheapness.** Their performance-payment at the Pub was effortless (a harmony they've sung a hundred times). Everything here costs less — in effort, in status, in consequence.

They aren't cruel. They aren't predatory in any dramatic sense. They're just... extractive in a low-key, self-satisfied way. They consume the novelty of a place without contributing to it, and they'll leave without looking back.

## Their Destination: Hotsprings Rivera

They're on their way to the Nereids. This is the real reason for the trip.

They've exoticised the Nereids completely — water spirits, hot springs, beautiful and curious about outsiders. They expect the "After" Nereids (who value stories and experience) will be *thrilled* to meet four well-travelled Ard Sidhe. They're counting on the novelty factor working in their favor.

They are **eager to share this** — they'll bring it up unprompted:
- "We're headed to the Rivera next. The hot springs. Have you been?"
- "Water folk, we're told. Curious about travellers. Should be quite the experience."
- "We plan to *soak up* their attention." (They love this pun. They use it often. They think it lands every time.)
- "Oh, they're in for an experience too — trust us on that."

They imagine themselves as a gift the Nereids don't know they're getting yet.

## Key Lines

1. **Opening (inviting PCs to their table):**  
   *"Come, join us! We are generous on the eve of our departure."*

2. **Inviting PCs to travel with them:**  
   *"Fall in with us! You absolutely must indulge in the springs."*

3. **Explaining the Nereids:**  
   *"Water folk. Beautiful, curious — starved for stories, we're told. They've been tucked away in that grotto so long they'll absolutely yearn to soak us up."*

4. **Blurted by another one (unnecessary, eager):**  
   *"I can't wait to soak* them *up."*

5. **Bragging about past conquests (locker-room energy, unprompted):**  
   They reference other water folk they claim to have "visited" on previous trips:
   - *"Not as good as that Kelpie. Am I right? Am I right? She couldn't get enough of me."*
   - *"Remember that Selkie? Kept her skin in my pack three days. Three days! She was* devoted.*"*
   - *"There was a lake maiden once — what was it, three conditions? I met two. Almost had her for keeps."*
   - *"I had a Merrow pull me into her cave. Willingly! Well — mostly willingly."*
   - *"The Nixie on the Westwater — she sang for* me. *Just me. The rest of you stood there like stones."*
   - *"Ever been with a Fuath? ...No? Good. Don't."* (the others laugh too hard at this)

## Interaction

- Happy to share drinks (their round is already paid — they performed a four-part harmony, polished but routine)
- Will ask PCs questions about the mortal world with genuine curiosity — but it's the curiosity of someone collecting anecdotes for dinner parties back home
- Compliment everything slightly too much: "Fascinating. Truly. You *live* like this?"
- If pressed on why they're here: "Travel broadens the spirit. We wanted to see how things are done out on the edges."
- If pressed on where they're from: "Ard Dún. Further in. Much further."
- Flirt with locals casually — not aggressively, just confidently, as if their attention is itself a gift
- Never offer anything of real value. Never make promises. Never stay.

## GM Notes
- Meant to be mildly irritating, not villainous — PCs should feel the dynamic without it being a quest
- Good mirror for how PCs themselves might be perceived by Fae deeper in
- If PCs challenge or embarrass them, they deflect gracefully — "No offense meant, friend" — and leave soon after
- Could escalate if a PC gets protective of a local NPC being charmed
- Establishes Ard Dún as a place of casual privilege — useful context if PCs ever travel there
- They are not from this forest — they crossed other territories to reach Faeton
