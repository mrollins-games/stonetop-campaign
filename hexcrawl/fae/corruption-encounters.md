# Corruption Encounters — Introduction by Depth

> Three encounters designed to introduce the corrupted Fae threat gradually. At Depth 1, it's dismissible. At Depth 2, a pattern emerges. At Depth 3, the PCs see the machine.

---

## Depth 1 — "The Quiet Clearing"

**When:** Travel or exploration in Depth 1. Best placed after the PCs have already experienced a normal, lively Faedom scene (the Singing Rade, Faeton, etc.) so the contrast lands.

**Read/paraphrase:**

> You come to a clearing. Nothing is wrong, exactly. The grass is green. The trees are standing. But it's quiet — not the quiet of dawn or dusk, the quiet of a room someone just left.

**What the PCs find:**

- A ring of six or seven trees. Two of them have been **girdled** — bark stripped in a clean circle around the trunk, about waist height. The cuts are precise. Not animal damage. Not wind.
- The girdled trees are still alive but they won't be for long. Sap bleeds dark down the trunk.
- The grass inside the ring is **flattened in one direction**, as though something heavy was dragged toward the deeper forest.
- A **splash of dark liquid** on a rock near the edge. Not blood. Thicker. Doesn't smell like anything — that's what's wrong about it. Everything in the Faedom smells like *something*.

**If the PCs investigate:**
- The dark liquid is ichor. They won't know that word yet. It's cold to the touch and slightly oily. Doesn't wash off easily. Stains skin grey for a day.
- The drag marks lead south (deeper) and fade within a few hundred yards.
- A Fae NPC encountered afterward (Mivvi, a traveler, a merchant) shrugs it off if told: *"Trees die. Even here."* No one's worried yet.

**If the PCs taste/touch the ichor:** Faint nausea. A flash of something — not a vision, more a *feeling*. Stillness. The absence of wind. Like remembering a room with no doors.

**The point:** This is dismissible. Two damaged trees and some weird sap. Not worth derailing for. The PCs file it away and move on.

---

## Depth 2 — "The Uprooted Grove"

**When:** Travel in Depth 2. Best placed after the PCs have been in the Faedom long enough to understand that trees here are *alive* in a way mortal trees aren't — they have names, they respond, they matter.

**Read/paraphrase:**

> The trail opens into a wide depression — a shallow bowl in the forest floor, maybe forty feet across. It's full of trees. Or it was. Every tree in the bowl has been **pulled from the ground**.

**What the PCs find:**

- Fifteen to twenty trees, **uprooted and laid on their sides** in rough parallel lines. Roots exposed, still tangled with dark soil. Some of the roots are still twitching — slow, weak grasping motions, like fingers closing on nothing.
- The holes where the trees stood are **filled with dark liquid**. Ichor. Small pools of it, still and perfectly flat. No reflection. The canopy above should be visible in the surface but isn't.
- The soil around the pools is **grey**. Not brown, not black. Grey. Nothing grows in the grey patches. Not even moss.
- One tree at the edge of the grove is still standing but **wrapped in something**. Dark vines? No — tendrils of dried ichor, like someone poured it over the trunk and it hardened into veins. The tree is alive inside but can't move. Its leaves are curled inward.

**If the PCs investigate:**
- Anyone with a connection to nature/spirits (Wolfram especially) feels this place like a wound. The trees had names. They don't anymore. The names are *gone*, not forgotten — removed.
- The ichor pools are deeper than they look. Drop a stick in — it sinks further than the hole should allow.
- **Tracks:** Multiple sets, barefoot, humanoid but wrong. Toes too long. Gait too even — no variation in stride, as though they all walk at exactly the same pace.
- Following the tracks leads to more uprooted areas. This isn't one incident. It's a **campaign**. Systematic. Organized.

**The pattern clicks:** This is what the girdled trees at Depth 1 were the beginning of. Someone is killing the forest on purpose. Not randomly — *methodically*.

**If the PCs touch the ichor-wrapped tree:** They hear it. Not words. A vibration through the bark — a low, continuous tone like a bell struck underwater. It's trying to ring its own name and can't. If Wolfram has his Naming Seed, it goes cold in his pocket.

**Encounter — Ichored Fae:**

While investigating, the PCs spot **two figures** at the far edge of the grove. Fae. Standing perfectly still, facing the treeline, not moving.

- They look like normal Fae from behind — roughly humanoid, dressed in forest materials.
- When approached or addressed, they **turn slowly**. Their faces are *right* but *still*. No micro-expressions. No breathing. Eyes open, unblinking. Skin has a faint grey undertone.
- They speak, but only in **statements**. Never questions. Never responses to what you said — just declarations:
  - *"The roots go too deep."*
  - *"It will be better when it's still."*
  - *"We are helping."*
  - *"You should rest. Everything should rest."*
- They are not aggressive unless the PCs interfere with the grove (try to replant a tree, drain a pool, free the wrapped tree). Then they move — fast, wrong, like something pulling them on strings — and try to restrain, not kill. They want to drag you to the pools.

**Stats:** Use Fae stat blocks but: immune to fear, don't react to pain, don't stop when injured. They stop only when physically unable to move.

## Depth 3 — "The Un-Well"

**When:** Deep exploration in Depth 3. This is the payoff. By now the PCs should be actively looking for the source.

**Read/paraphrase:**

> You smell it before you see it. Not a smell, exactly — the *absence* of smell. A dead zone in the air. Then the trees thin and you see it: a structure, rising above the canopy, made of things that should not have been built with.

**Setting the scene:**

The Un-Well is a **tower built of uprooted trees**, stacked and woven trunk-over-trunk, roots outward. Forty feet tall, maybe more. It looks like a nest built by something that hates nests. The roots bristle outward in every direction — dried, grey, still.

At the top, the structure curves inward like a cupped hand. **Ichor drips from the peak** in a slow, steady stream — not pouring, just dripping. One drop every few seconds. Each drop catches light wrong. Each drop falls into a **pool at the base**, perfectly round, ten feet across, black and still.

Around the Un-Well:

- **The baptism pools.** Six or seven smaller depressions arranged in a rough circle, each filled with ichor. Some are empty — recently used. Dark stains on the edges where something (someone) was held down and submerged.
- **Corrupted Fae.** A dozen or more, going about tasks with mechanical calm. Some carry uprooted saplings toward the structure. Some tend the pools — skimming the surface, though nothing floats on it. Some stand at the perimeter, facing outward, watching. All move at the same pace.
- **A captive.** One uncorrupted Fae, bound in dried ichor-tendrils, near a pool. Alive. Terrified. Silent — not because they choose to be, but because the air near the pools swallows sound. Their mouth moves but nothing comes out.
- **No birds. No insects. No wind.** The area around the Un-Well is the stillness the corrupted want for everything. A preview of their finished world.

**The Un-Well itself:**

- The ichor comes from *above*. Not from inside the structure — from the sky. The tower is a **collector**, not a source. It's shaped to catch something that drips from... where? The Faedom has no sky in the mortal sense. Whatever is above is leaking, and the corrupted built this to gather it.
- The base pool is the **mother pool**. All other baptism pools were filled from this one. Destroying or draining the base pool doesn't stop the dripping — but it stops the corrupted from making more of themselves. At least here. At least for now.
- The structure hums. Not audibly — in the bones. Stand near it too long and your teeth ache. Wolfram's acorn shrieks. Halpin's ring goes dark. Ted's stamp is warm — almost burning — as though it wants to *rule* on this. Declare it wrong. Seal it shut.

**If the PCs free the captive:**
The Fae gasps, coughs, speaks in a rush: *"They don't kill you. That's not what this is. They make you agree. You stand in the pool and something asks you a question and the answer is always the same and then you don't want to leave. I said no. They're waiting for me to change my mind. They're very patient."*

**What was the question?**
*"Would you like to rest?"*

**If the PCs attack the Un-Well:**
The corrupted Fae converge — not charging, *walking*. All at the same pace. All from every direction. They don't shout, don't rage, don't threaten. They just come. And keep coming. And if you destroy part of the structure, the dripping continues. The ichor falls on the rubble and begins to pool again. The structure isn't the source. The *hole in the sky* is the source.

**If the PCs look up:**
Above the Un-Well, high above, where the Faedom's canopy should close — there's a gap. Not a hole. A *crack*. Something dark and wet glistens beyond it. The Faedom's ceiling is thin here, and what's on the other side is leaking through.

## GM Notes

- **Depth 1** should feel like set dressing. Don't push it. Let the players notice or not.
- **Depth 2** should feel like finding a crime scene. The emotional hit comes from the trees being *someone*, not just something.
- **Depth 3** should feel like walking into the enemy's base camp and realizing they're not hiding. They're not afraid of you. They're not even angry. They just want you to stop moving.
- The corrupted Fae ARE villains. The Things Below's influence is evil — full stop. Once converted, they are lost. Don't soften this. They destroy, they corrupt, they drag others into the pools. Whatever they were before is gone.
- The Un-Well is not the final threat. It's an outpost. There are more. The crack in the sky is the real problem — and that's a later session.
- Pacing: these three encounters should be spread across **at least two sessions**, probably three. Don't rush the escalation.
