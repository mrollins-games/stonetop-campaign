# Fae Laws

## Foundation — The Nature of Fae (from source)

These aren't laws someone wrote down. They are the nature of the Fae — as inescapable as gravity.

### Promises
- A Fae **cannot break a promise or an oath**, but they are keen at finding loopholes.
- When a mortal **fails to uphold a promise made to a Fae**, either they become beholden to that Fae or the Fae takes it as an insult (Fae's choice).
- **Death voids all debts and promises** — passing through the Last Door releases both sides.

### Debts
- Fae feel the weight of debts and insults as a **physical thing**, like heavy chains.
- When a **Fae is beholden to you**, you have advantage on rolls to resist or defend against it.
- When you **leverage a debt they owe**, gain advantage to Persuade or influence them.
- When **you are beholden to a Fae**, you have disadvantage on rolls made directly against them.
- Debts can be **traded to another Fae**.

### Insults & Etiquette
One might insult a Fae by:
- Rudely refusing an offer
- Failing to honor a debt or promise
- Taking what they think they own
- Making demands/threats without proper leverage
- Sneaking iron into their presence
- Failing to play along with their games
- Disparaging what they value about themselves
- Accusing them of any of the above

An insulted Fae might: demand satisfaction, storm off, offer forgiveness as a gift (creating a debt), ignore all other etiquette, spread rumors, hold a grudge, or resort to violence.

### Iron
- Iron (including steel) is **offensive** to Fae — its presence insults them.
- Iron disrupts their magic, burns their skin, ignores their Armor, leaves permanent scars.
- Iron can truly kill a Fae dead.
- **Black iron** is worse: unravels magic, suppurates flesh (+1d6 damage, messy), wounds never heal, eventually creates Hollow Fae.

### Glamour & Illusion
- Illusions are lies with no substance.
- Glamour changes a thing and how it interacts with the world — wounds from glamoured weapons are real.
- Glamours must "rhyme" — poetic transformations only.
- Fae see through each other's illusions/glamours but play along (doing otherwise is insulting).
- Iron/steel causes illusions to flicker. Black iron dispels both illusions and glamour.

## Territorial Laws — Custom (this campaign)

Laws enforced within Fae territory, either by custom, by the Chieftains, or by the territory itself.

### The Law of Guests
- **A traveler who accepts hospitality is a guest.** A guest may not be harmed by their host, nor may they harm their host. Lasts until they leave.
- **A guest who overstays is no longer a guest.** How long is "too long"? The host decides.
- **Refusing offered hospitality is not an insult** — but refusing *three times* is.

### The Law of Paths
- **A path taken willingly leads where it will.** You chose to walk — you accept where it goes.
- **A path taken under duress does not bind.** If tricked or forced onto a path, debts incurred along it are void.
- **Blocking a path is a declaration.** It means "this is mine, and you are not welcome." Respond accordingly.

### The Law of Names
- **To name a thing is to claim knowledge of it.** Speaking a Fae's name aloud in their territory signals you know them — and they will know you spoke.
- **A true name spoken commands.** If true names exist, using one is the gravest act — war-justification.
- **Mortals may offer a false name without insult**, but if caught in the lie, they are beholden.

### The Law of Gifts
- **A gift freely given creates no debt** — but the giver earns goodwill, which is remembered.
- **A gift with conditions is not a gift; it is a deal.** Treat it as such.
- **Refusing a gift is rude but not an insult.** Refusing and then *taking* it later — that's an insult.

### The Law of Contest
- **Any challenge accepted must be seen through.** Walking away mid-contest is forfeit.
- **The terms must be stated before the contest begins.** Changing terms mid-contest voids the outcome.
- **A victor's claim is absolute** — within the stated terms. No more, no less.

### The Law of Seasons
- **Each Chieftain holds authority in their season.** Spring: the Warbler. Summer: the Crowned Stag. Autumn: Longshadow. Winter: the Bear.
- **In their season, a Chieftain's word is territorial law.** Other Chieftains defer (grudgingly).
- **Between seasons, authority is contested.** The equinoxes and solstices are dangerous, unpredictable times.

## Unwritten Understandings

These aren't laws. They're just how things work.

- **Nothing is free.** If it seems free, you haven't noticed the cost yet.
- **Fae do not lie.** But they mislead, omit, reframe, imply, and let you deceive yourself.
- **Mortal time means nothing here.** Urgency is a mortal problem. Fae find it amusing.
- **Violence is vulgar.** Most Fae prefer to win without it. Those who resort to violence are either desperate or making a statement.
- **Memory is currency.** What you know, what you've seen, what you remember — all tradeable. All valuable. All vulnerable.
