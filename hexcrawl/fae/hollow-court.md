# The Hollow Court — Encounter

> **POI #11** — Depth 1 — Remnants of the War  
> **Theme:** Justice, hospitality, cohabitation  
> **Tone:** Whimsical test disguised as a petty dispute

---

## Approach

The PCs push through **aggressive flora** — thorned creepers, grasping vines, branches that swing back unprompted. Not dangerous, but persistent. Requires effort (a few Defy Danger rolls, hacking with blades, or patience).

When they break through, they see it:

**A giant tree.** Ancient. Wider than a house. A **spiral staircase** winds around its exterior — carved from the tree itself, worn smooth by centuries of use. The staircase ascends into the upper canopy and out of sight.

Below, at the tree's base, the roots form a **hollow opening** — an archway into darkness beneath. It's hard to see inside, but the space is enormous. At least the size of a banquet hall.

From inside: **two voices, bickering.** Soft but angry. Speaking over each other.

---

## The Emergence

The voices stop — they've noticed the company.

Two figures emerge from the hollow:

1. **A black fuzzy spider**, half the size of a man. Round body, amber-gold eyes (four of them), white fuzzy "mittens" on her front two legs. Moves with surprising delicacy.

2. **A young woman** — skin as absent of color as the cave behind her. Void-black, not brown. White hair pulled up in a bun. Red eyes. Perky sharp ears. Wearing a purple and silver outfit with white fur trim. Spider-motif jewelry at her throat.

As they exit, the **roots behind them shift** — pulling together from either side of the passage like a closing jaw. The hollow seals shut behind them.

They immediately start at it again, making their claims to the PCs:

---

## The Dispute

> **The Spider:** "I've been nesting here! I came when no one was here and I've been tending this place for years!"

> **The Woman:** "I wish to bed here as well! My ancestors lived here! This is my birthright!"

> **The Spider:** "Who's to know if that's even true! No one was here when I arrived!"

They stop. Between them, the bark of the great tree shifts — a **face** emerges. Wooden features, ancient, impassive. It speaks in a flat, even tone:

> **The Tree:** "You hold court here."

Each makes their case again, now formally:

- **The Spider** claims tenure — she arrived to an empty place, has spun her webs, maintained the space, tended the tree for years. Labor and presence.
- **The Woman** claims heritage — her ancestors lived here, it is her right by blood and history. She adds, turning to the PCs: *"If you side with me, you may stay in comfort. I'll prepare proper bedding for you."*

The PCs must judge.

---

## Outcomes

### If PCs side with the Woman (comfort / bribe)

The Tree's face speaks: **"And so it is."**

The ruse stops. Both the spider and the woman drop the act — disappointment on their faces. They reveal the truth: they both live here already, happily, tending the tree together. They were testing the PCs.

The hollow **remains closed**. The PCs are not shown inside. They may rest at the base of the tree (uncomfortable, exposed). The spider and woman retreat up the staircase without offering more.

**Consequence:** The PCs chose comfort over justice. The Court does not recognize them. They can return later and try again — but the tree remembers.

### If PCs side with the Spider (merit / labor)

All are pleased. The Tree's face speaks: **"And so it is."**

The ruse stops — but joyfully. The spider and the woman embrace (the spider wraps one fuzzy leg around the woman's shoulder). They reveal the truth: they both live here, happily, as partners. They were testing whether the PCs could judge on merit rather than be bought.

The **hollow reopens** — roots pulling apart to reveal the amphitheater within. The PCs are welcomed inside for a tour. Later, the spider spins them hammocks in a nook of the tree while the woman lines them with soft white furs. **Best rest in the Faedom.**

**Consequence:** The Court recognizes the PCs as fair judges. This has implications for future encounters — Fae who learn of this will treat them with more respect regarding matters of law and debt.

---

## The Tour — Inside the Hollow Court

If the PCs earn entry, the spider and the woman give a tour:

The hollow beneath the roots is a **natural amphitheater**. Root-ridges form curved bench rows descending toward a central floor. The ceiling is a web of interlocking roots — stabilized and decorated with the spider's silk (glowing faintly, catching jar-light). The woman has strung **lanterns** — glass jars with fireflies or luminescent moss — on silk lines between the root-pillars.

**What they see:**

- **The Benches:** Curved root-rows, enough seating for two dozen. Worn smooth in the seats, rough where no one has sat in centuries. Some seats have symbols carved into them — sigils of the old Court members.
- **The Central Floor:** A flat stone surface, slightly concave. Acoustic — even a whisper from the center carries to every seat. The spider explains: "Cases were heard here. Both parties stood in the middle."
- **The Tree's Face:** Visible from inside too, above the "stage." It is the arbiter's position. The face is dormant now (eyes closed, features smooth) unless addressed.
- **Decorations:** The spider's webs catch light beautifully — practical AND aesthetic. The woman has placed furs and cushions on some seats, mushroom arrangements, small offerings of food on the carved sigil-seats (honoring absent members).
- **The Atmosphere:** Cozy despite the grandeur. These two have made a courthouse into a home — without destroying what it was.

**What they learn (in conversation):**

- The Court was an assembly of Fae convened to rule on **ambiguous cases** — disputes where existing Fae law didn't clearly apply.
- The Court's members were powerful Fae from different factions, chosen for wisdom (or at least for balance).
- The Court **has not assembled in a very long time.** Neither the spider nor the woman will say exactly why — they get quiet, exchange a glance. "A ruling was made. It was... too broad. It hurt people who shouldn't have been hurt. The members couldn't face each other after that."
- The tree is the **Court's arbiter** — it declares outcomes but does not argue cases. It only speaks when a case is presented and judged.
- The tree also **keeps records** of every ruling it has declared. The spider mentions this casually: "It remembers everything. Every case, every outcome. It's all up there somewhere." She gestures vaguely toward the spiral staircase.

**What they DON'T volunteer:**
- What the final ruling was about (Forest Folk imprisonment)
- What's up the spiral staircase (the archive)
- Who the Court members were specifically

PCs can investigate further by:
- Climbing the spiral staircase on their own (finding the archive — leaf-records of every ruling)
- Asking the tree directly ("Do you remember the last case?")
- Asking other Fae NPCs elsewhere who know the Court's history

## NPCs

### The Spider — Mivvi

**Solitary, tiny (for a Fae), magical, stealthy, devious**  
HP 8; Armor 1 (dense silk)  
Damage: bite d6 (hand, piercing) — but she'd rather not  
Special qualities: Fae nature; silk production (structural, decorative, or binding); can climb any surface; sees in total darkness; webs vibrate to detect lies spoken within them

**Instinct:** to tend and protect what she's built

**Moves:**
- Spin a web so beautiful it distracts from its purpose (trapping, binding, recording)
- Detect a lie through vibration — her webs tremble differently when someone speaks false
- Offer cozy hospitality that's hard to leave (hammocks, warmth, soft light)
- Scuttle away faster than expected — she's shy when startled
- Wrap something precious in silk for safekeeping (a gift, a secret, an injury)
- Know every crack, tunnel, and hidden space in the tree — she mapped it all

**Personality:**
- Soft-spoken, a little anxious, deeply proud of her work
- Speaks about her webs the way a craftsman speaks about their best piece
- Gets flustered by compliments but clearly wants them
- Protective of the woman and the tree — bristles if either is disrespected
- Uses phrases like "I've been keeping this" and "it was empty when I came" — themes of finding purpose in abandoned places

**Appearance:** Round black body the size of a large dog. Dense velvety fur. Four amber eyes that catch light like lanterns. White fuzzy patches on her front legs ("mittens"). Moves with surprising gentleness for her size — places each leg deliberately, as if afraid of breaking something.

---

### The Woman — Sable

**Solitary, fae, magical, charming**  
HP 12; Armor 2 (natural resilience)  
Damage: d8 (close, cold touch — drains warmth)  
Special qualities: Fae nature; darkvision; cold aura (subtle — you notice your breath misting); ancestral memory (fragments of what her forebears experienced here); unnervingly still when not speaking

**Instinct:** to reclaim and honor what was lost

**Moves:**
- Invoke ancestral right — speak with the weight of generations behind her claim
- Offer comfort that comes with a subtle test attached
- Go completely still — unnervingly motionless — when listening or judging
- Know a fragment of old Court history that she shouldn't (ancestral bleed-through)
- Make the darkness feel welcoming rather than threatening (she belongs to it)
- Produce white fur garments or bedding seemingly from nowhere ("I always have extra")

**Personality:**
- Warm but precise — every sentence feels slightly rehearsed, like court speech softened into conversation
- Genuinely affectionate toward Mivvi — touches her fuzzy leg absently while talking
- Claims heritage often but with more pride than entitlement (she's earned her place too)
- Gets wistful when discussing the Court's history — she feels connected to it personally
- Uses phrases like "my people built this" and "we've always been here" — themes of belonging and legacy

**Appearance:** Humanoid, medium height. Skin is void-black — not a shade of brown but a true absence of reflected light, like looking into the hollow behind her. White hair in a loose bun. Red eyes (not angry — just their color, like garnets). Pointed ears, sharp and expressive. Wears purple and silver with white fur trim. Spider-motif silver necklace. Moves with feline grace — economical, deliberate.

---

### The Court Tree — The Arbiter

**Solitary, immobile, ancient, magical**  
HP —; Armor — (it's a tree — it doesn't fight)  
Damage: — (it doesn't attack)  
Special qualities: Fae nature; records all rulings spoken in its presence; face manifests only when court is held; spiral staircase is part of its body; root-system extends beneath the entire amphitheater; archive of leaf-records stored in upper canopy

**Instinct:** to witness and declare

**Moves:**
- Manifest its face when a dispute is formally presented (cannot be forced — it decides when something counts as a case)
- Declare "and so it is" — making a ruling final and binding within Fae law
- Seal or open the hollow (roots respond to its will)
- Remain silent when no case is before it (cannot be baited into conversation outside of formal proceedings)
- Produce a leaf-record of any past ruling if asked directly and sincerely ("Do you remember...?")
- Remember everything — every case, every word, every outcome. It never forgets and it never edits.

**Personality:**
- **No personality.** It is not friendly, hostile, or neutral. It is a mechanism. A function. It witnesses. It declares. That's all.
- Speaks only in short declarative sentences. Never explains. Never elaborates.
- Its face is carved-looking — wooden features, closed eyes that don't open, a mouth that moves only for declarations.
- When not speaking, it is indistinguishable from a normal (if enormous) tree.

**Appearance:** A massive ancient tree — species unclear (aspects of oak, ash, and thorn simultaneously). The spiral staircase is grown from its body, not carved. The face is a vertical oval of smoother bark on the trunk's south side, features visible only when active. Root system is vast — the entire amphitheater is contained within its root network.

---

## Connections

- **Up the staircase:** The tree's archive. Leaf-records of every ruling. PCs can discover this through exploration or conversation — it's not revealed automatically.
- **The last ruling:** Connects to the imprisonment of the Forest Folk. The Court voted to enforce judgment against corrupt Forest Folk; the spell was too broad and caught all of them. This caused the Court to never reassemble.
- **Future hooks:** If the Court recognizes the PCs as fair judges, other Fae may bring disputes TO them. The tree may manifest its face again if the PCs bring a case. The Court could potentially be reassembled — but that's a campaign-level event.

---

## GM Notes

- **The test is about bribery vs. merit.** The woman's offer of comfort is the trap. The spider's claim (labor, tenure, care) is the just answer. Make sure to emphasize the bribe clearly — "if you side with me, you can stay in comfort."
- **Don't make it obvious.** Play both arguments sincerely. The woman's heritage claim IS valid in Fae law. The spider's tenure claim IS valid too. The point is that the PCs shouldn't be swayed by what's in it for THEM.
- **The reveal should be warm, not punishing.** Even if PCs pick wrong, the disappointment is gentle — more "ah, shame" than hostile. They can try again later.
- **The tour is the reward.** Let PCs ask questions. Let Mivvi and Sable be charming hosts. Drop hints about the archive and the Court's history organically — don't infodump.
- **The archive is discoverable, not given.** PCs must either climb the stairs on their own, ask the tree directly, or hear about it from other NPCs later. Mivvi's offhand mention ("it's all up there somewhere") is the breadcrumb.
