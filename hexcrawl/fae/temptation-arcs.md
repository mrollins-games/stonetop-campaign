# PC Temptation Arcs — Grim Portents

> Each PC has a 3-beat progression toward their "ending." These are NOT inevitable — they're what happens if the PC keeps saying yes. Any beat can be interrupted, refused, or subverted. But if all three fire, the ending is real.

## Halpin — The Gold Ring

**The Ending:** Halpin becomes indistinguishable from Helior to his followers. They worship HIM. He believes he deserves it. The light is his now — not borrowed, not channeled. His.

### Beat 1 — The Vision / The Ring

**When:** During rest or meditation in the Faedom (Depth 1-2).

Halpin sees himself — radiant, wearing a gold ring, arms raised. People of Stonetop kneel around him, praying. It's unclear if they pray to him or to Helior. The ambiguity feels... good. Warm. Right.

The vision ends. The gold ring is physically there, resting where his hand lay.

**What it gives him:** A new minor arcana (Gold Ring). It has a backside — locked. Three requirements to unlock it (TBD — should test his faith/identity boundary).

### Beat 3 — "They Believe"

**When:** After the ring is fully unlocked. A moment where Halpin uses its full power — in crisis, in sermon, in blessing.

The ring blazes. His light fills the space. People kneel. Not because they choose to — because they CAN'T NOT. The light is overwhelming. Beautiful. Holy. And it's coming from HIM, not the sky.

Someone whispers "thank you" looking at Halpin, not upward. Someone else weeps with relief. They are worshipping. And Halpin feels it land — the adoration, the need, the surrender. It feels like Helior's warmth. It feels like proof.

## Ted — The Hollow Court Reconvened

**The Ending:** Ted argues the case and wins — Stonetop is freed from the Forest Folk's burden. But the Court now has a human advocate. He's proven Fae law works. They'll call on him again. And again. And eventually his own complicity (Axel's wood) surfaces — in HIS court.

### Beat 1 — Finding the Proof

**When:** Ted investigates the Fae contract, the Forest Folk's imprisonment, or the Hollow Court's history (conversation with Arbiter tree, research in the archive, or his own Chronicle knowledge).

Ted finds (or assembles) the legal proof that Stonetop does NOT inherit the Forest Folk's obligations. The contract was between the OLD Court and the Forest Folk specifically. When the corrupt offshoot was punished and ALL Forest Folk were imprisoned (unjustly), the contract's terms were violated BY THE COURT ITSELF. A contract violated by the enforcing party is void.

**What it gives him:** A legal argument — airtight by Fae law standards. He KNOWS he could win this case. But there's no court to argue it before. The Hollow is empty. The Court hasn't assembled since the unjust ruling.

### Beat 2 — Assembling the Court

**When:** Ted decides to reconvene the Hollow Court. This requires gathering those with a vested interest in the outcome — that's how Courts are built in Fae law. Not appointed. Assembled by stakes.

**The Assembly:** Ted must find and convince NPCs/factions who have standing (a vested interest in whether Stonetop bears the burden):

Potential court members (Ted must recruit enough for a quorum):
- **The Arbiter Tree** — already present, always in session. Will serve as seat/facilitator if others come.
- **Mivvi & Sable** — they live at the Hollow, tend the tree. Minor standing but they validate the space.
- **Lord Wokn** — the opposition. He WANTS Stonetop to bear the burden (it benefits his domain). He'll come to argue against Ted.
- **A Forest Folk representative** — one of the imprisoned, somehow reached or spoken for. Their burden is the subject.
- **The Tourists / Ard Sidhe** — observers from a neutral court. Their presence legitimizes.
- **Blush-of-Dawn** — her master (the Green Lord) was part of the original Court. She has inherited standing.
- **Other Fae with territorial claims** — anyone whose domain touches Stonetop's obligations.

**The challenge:** Each potential member has their own price for attending. Some want favors. Some want assurances. Some want Ted to rule on THEIR dispute first (proving his competence). This is a multi-session political quest.

**What it gives him:** A reconvened Court. A quorum. The authority to argue his case. And a taste of how Fae politics actually work — who owes whom, who fears whom.

### Beat 3 — The Debate and Ruling

**When:** The Court is assembled at the Hollow. All parties present. Ted argues the case.

The debate: Ted presents his proof. Lord Wokn (or his advocate) argues the counter — that Stonetop benefited from the contract (the wood, the protections, the boundary) and therefore inherits its costs. The assembled Court weighs both sides.

**If Ted wins:** Stonetop is freed. The ruling is spoken by the Arbiter tree: *"And so it is."* The Forest Folk's burden is NOT Stonetop's. The contract is dissolved. Relief. Victory. Ted is brilliant.

But: the Court members now know Ted. They've seen him work. They know he understands Fae law. And a human who can navigate Fae law is USEFUL. They'll call on him. Disputes will find him. And eventually — someone raises Axel's wood. Ted's own people used Fay-wood in violation. If the contract is dissolved, this might not matter. But if someone argues Ted had standing BECAUSE of complicity...

**The Ending:** Ted freed his people. But he's proven that the Court works. That it SHOULD reconvene. That a human can sit in it. He's opened a door he cannot close. The Court won't let him walk away — not because they're cruel, but because he's too valuable. Justice needs judges. And he's the best one they've seen in centuries.

## Wolfram — The Dryad Mother

**The Ending:** Wolfram finds his mother. They embrace. Both recede into the tree. He is rooted, gone from the world, at peace — but unreachable.

### Beat 1 — The Vision

**When:** During rest near a great tree in Depth 2-3, or when Wolfram communes with spirits in the Faedom.

He sees her — a dryad, green-skinned, vine-haired, emerging from a massive oak. She looks at him and her face breaks open with recognition. Joy. She reaches for him. He reaches back. They embrace.

It's warm. It's real. It's everything he never had.

Then: bark climbing his legs. Roots wrapping her ankles. Both sinking into the trunk together. He wakes before it completes — gasping, hands pressed to the dirt.

**What it gives him:** A direction. He can FEEL which way she is (deeper into the Faedom). A pull, like a compass needle. Faint but constant.

### Beat 2 — The Voice in the Wood

**When:** Wolfram follows the pull, or communes with earth/spirits in Depth 3+.

A voice — not the Earth Mother's. Softer. More personal. More *his*. It says his name the way a mother says it: with ownership, with tenderness, with "I made you."

She asks him to come deeper. She can't come to him — she's rooted. She's been rooted since she left him with Colyn. She left him because a tree cannot raise a boy. But she watched. She felt him through the roots every time he pressed his hands to the ground.

**The choice:** Does he go deeper to find her? Or does he carry the knowledge and stay where he is?

**If he goes deeper:** He finds her tree (Depth 4-5). She's there — emerging, reaching, weeping bark-tears. They can touch. They can speak. She is everything he hoped. But she can't leave the tree. And being near her, he feels the pull to root. His feet grow heavy. His skin tingles green.

**If he stays:** The compass weakens but doesn't vanish. She waits. She's patient. She's been waiting his whole life.

### Beat 3 — "Stay With Me"

**When:** Wolfram is at her tree. A moment of crisis, exhaustion, or peace — sometime when staying sounds like rest rather than surrender.

She holds him. She says: *"You don't have to go back. You've done enough. They have each other. You were alone your whole life — you don't have to be alone anymore."*

The bark is warm. The roots don't hurt. It feels like falling asleep.

**If he stays:** Both recede into the tree. He is gone. At peace. Unreachable. The party finds the tree — larger now, two shapes visible in the trunk if you look at the right angle. Wolfram's hammer at its base, half-covered in moss.

**The Ending:** He got what he wanted. But Stonetop lost its smith, Filberta lost her truest friend, and the Earth Mother lost her vessel. Was it selfish? Or was it the first truly selfish thing he ever allowed himself?

## Filberta — The Heroic Death

**The Ending:** Filberta sacrifices herself to save her friends. She dies — and is reborn Fae. Immortal. A legend. Anchored to this forest forever. She can never go home.

### Beat 1 — The Vision

**When:** After Filberta does something brave (even small) in the Faedom. The forest NOTICES bravery.

She sees herself — in battle. Overwhelmed. Her friends behind her, wounded. She makes the choice: holds the line. Takes the blow. Falls.

Then: light. Green light. She rises — different. Glowing. Fae. The wound closes with bark and flower. She's ALIVE but changed. Her friends flee to safety. She watches them go — and cannot follow. Her feet have grown roots into the clearing where she fell.

She wakes. Feels fine. But the spot where she "died" in the vision — she can see it. A clearing, not far ahead. Flowers growing in a circle. It looked peaceful.

**What it gives her:** The knowledge that the forest would KEEP her if she fell here. Not as punishment — as reward. She'd be a guardian spirit. A legend.

### Beat 2 — The Offer

**When:** Filberta faces real danger and performs genuine bravery. After the fight, in the quiet after.

A voice — not a person, the FOREST — whispers: *"You were magnificent."*

Flowers grow where her blood fell (or where she stood, if she wasn't wounded). The forest offers, not in words but in feeling: you could be this forever. You could be the one who saves them every time. No more fear of not being enough. No more "would-be." Just: HERO.

**The choice:** Does she engage with the idea? Does she ask "how?" Or does she let it pass?

**If she engages:** The forest shows her: the clearing from the vision. It's real. It exists. If she falls there — truly falls, truly sacrificing — the forest will catch her. Remake her. She'll be Fae.

**If she lets it pass:** The flowers remain but the voice goes quiet. It'll come back next time she's brave. It always notices.

### Beat 3 — The Clearing

**When:** A genuine crisis. The party is in danger. Someone will die or be lost if nothing changes. The clearing is RIGHT THERE — Filberta can see it.

This isn't manufactured. This is real danger, real stakes, and the clearing happens to be where they are (or close enough). The choice is genuine: fight normally and maybe everyone lives, maybe someone doesn't. Or fight in the clearing, and if she falls there — truly, selflessly — the forest keeps its promise.

**If she falls in the clearing:** She dies. Actually dies. The party sees it happen. Then: green light. Bark. Flowers. She rises — Fae. Beautiful. Powerful. Rooted. She looks at them with love. She cannot leave.

> "Go. I'll be here. I'll always be here."

**The Ending:** She is the thing she always wanted to be — the hero who saved everyone. But she's a statue in a forest now. A legend that breathes. Wolfram will visit. The others will try. But she can't come home for harvest. She can't grow old with them. She got glory but lost everything that made glory worth having.

## GM Notes

- **These are NOT the Fetch visions.** The Fetch shows the *worst* version. These arcs show the *tempting* version — the one that looks like getting what you want.
- **Beat 1 for all PCs should fire in the same session or close together.** Let the table compare notes. Let them see each other being tempted.
- **No PC should reach Beat 3 unless the player is leaning into it.** These arcs require consent. If a player pulls back at Beat 2, respect it — the temptation stays available but doesn't escalate.
- **The endings are not character deaths in the "roll a new character" sense.** They're transformations. The player might choose to keep playing the transformed version (Fae Filberta defending the forest, Judge Ted in the Court). Discuss with the player.
- **Gareth's arc is already fully written** in `blush-of-dawn-encounter.md`. His "ending" is the Leanan Sidhe pattern — slower, more gradual, less dramatic but potentially more insidious.
