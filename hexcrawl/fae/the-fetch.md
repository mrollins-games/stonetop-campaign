# The Fetch — Encounter

> **Type:** Camp / Travel encounter — individual visions  
> **Depth:** Any (Depth 2+ recommended)  
> **Theme:** Temptation, identity, foreshadowing what each Fae offer actually costs  
> **Tone:** Deeply unsettling — not hostile, not a fight. A mirror.  
> **Trigger:** Deploy after the party has met at least 2-3 Fae NPCs who could tempt them. Each PC sees their OWN double — showing what they become if they accept a specific Fae's boon.

---

## What Is a Fetch?

A Fetch is your exact double, seen where you shouldn't be. In folklore it's a death omen — here it's more specific: it's a **future self**. A version of you that said yes. It isn't real (yet). It isn't hostile. It's just... there. Looking at you. Being what you might become.

The Fetch doesn't attack. Doesn't speak (unless the PC engages). Doesn't chase. It simply exists, undeniably, for a few terrible seconds. Then it's gone.

---

## How to Deploy

**Option A — All at once (campfire)**  
During a night watch, each PC on watch sees their Fetch simultaneously — standing at the tree line, watching the camp. Different positions. All looking inward. Then gone.

**Option B — One per session (breadcrumb)**  
Space them out. One PC per session sees theirs — on the trail ahead, reflected in water, standing where they were a moment ago. The others don't see it. The target PC is alone in the experience.

**Option C — One traumatic moment**  
All five Fetches are seen by the WHOLE party — standing in a clearing in a circle, as if in conversation. The party walks into what looks like themselves from behind. Then they turn around. Then they vanish.

## The Five Fetches

### Gareth's Fetch — The Muse's Prize

**Which Fae:** Blush-of-Dawn  
**The Boon:** Answers. Every mystery resolves without effort. He knows where things are, what happened, why — without searching.

**What He Sees:**

Himself — but *still*. Completely, unnervingly still. Gareth's Fetch sits against a tree with open eyes, staring at nothing. Books and scrolls surround him but they're all closed. The Azure Hand rests across his lap, dormant — not tingling, not active. His face is placid. Content. Empty.

He isn't searching for anything. He already knows it all. He's waiting for the next revelation to wash over him because effort has become meaningless. His eyes are bright — too bright. The brightness of someone running a fever they don't feel.

He's thin. Not starving-thin. Just... lessened. Like someone removed 10% of him evenly.

**The Tell:** If Gareth looks closely — his Fetch's hands have never been dirty. No ink stains. No charcoal. No evidence of WORK. The knowledge came free. Nothing was earned.

---

### Halpin's Fetch — The Prophet Consumed

**Which Fae:** Blush-of-Dawn (or the Faedom's beauty itself — the light at Depth 1)  
**The Boon:** Perfect clarity of faith. Visions so vivid they become sermons that move crowds to tears.

**What He Sees:**

Himself — radiant. Literally glowing. Halpin's Fetch stands with arms slightly raised, face tilted upward as if basking in something only he can see. A small crowd of shadowy figures kneels around him — worshipping. They have no faces. They don't need them — they exist only as audience.

He looks magnificent. He looks holy. He looks like he hasn't eaten in weeks. His ribs are visible through his shirt. His eyes are open but unfocused — staring at the vision behind reality, never at anything real. He doesn't blink.

**The Tell:** The faceless crowd is wearing Stonetop clothing. These are his people. They're kneeling because they can't look away. Not because they chose to.

---

### Wolfram's Fetch — The Serene Hermit

**Which Fae:** The Oath-Keeper (#11) or Blush-of-Dawn  
**The Boon:** His patron's voice becomes constant, intimate, warm. The loneliness of devotion replaced with perfect communion.

**What He Sees:**

Himself — smiling. Genuinely, peacefully smiling. Wolfram's Fetch sits in a clearing, alone, looking content in a way the real Wolfram has never achieved. No tension in his shoulders. No urgency. No burden. He's conversing — mouth moving, eyes bright, head tilting as if listening to a response — but there's no one there.

He's talking to his patron. Constantly. He doesn't need anyone else. He doesn't look AT Wolfram. Doesn't notice him. Doesn't notice anything external at all.

**The Tell:** Around the Fetch, the grass has died in a circle. Not corrupted — just ignored. The world around him has stopped mattering. He's been sitting here for a long time. No one came looking for him. Or they did, and he didn't look up.

---

### Ted's Fetch — The Law Made Flesh

**Which Fae:** The Hollow Court (Arbiter tree) or Lord Wokn  
**The Boon:** Every ruling is obviously correct. No one argues. No ambiguity. Perfect law from his mouth.

**What He Sees:**

Himself — standing at a crossroads. Perfectly upright. Hands behind his back. Face neutral. Ted's Fetch is judging something — two shadowy figures stand before him, clearly in dispute. The Fetch speaks a single word. Both figures bow and separate. No argument. No deliberation. Just... verdict.

He looks gaunt. Brittle. His jawline is sharper than it should be. His eyes are clear and cold — the warmth that makes Ted's rulings humane is gone. What's left is correct, efficient, and completely hollow.

**The Tell:** The two figures bowed — but they didn't AGREE. They obeyed. There's a difference. One of them is trembling. Ted's Fetch doesn't notice. Doesn't need to.

---

### Filberta's Fetch — The Legend Without Self

**Which Fae:** The Race Track (POI #15) or Blush-of-Dawn  
**The Boon:** Every swing lands. Every leap sticks. She becomes the hero she imagines.

**What She Sees:**

Herself — mid-stride, mid-action, mid-glory. Filberta's Fetch is in motion — sword raised, foot planted on a fallen enemy, expression fierce and triumphant. She looks EXACTLY like the hero Filberta wants to be. Perfect form. Perfect courage. Legendary.

But look at her face. Really look. She's smiling — but it's the smile of someone performing. Her eyes are scanning for audience. The enemy at her feet doesn't matter. The deed doesn't matter. Only whether someone is watching.

**The Tell:** The fallen enemy she's standing on? Look closer. It's wearing HER clothes. Her old clothes. The person she was before the boon. She's standing on her own past self and posing.

---

## If a PC Engages Their Fetch

If someone approaches their Fetch, speaks to it, or touches it:

**Touches it:** Cold. Solid for one second — then it dissolves like morning mist burned off by sun. The PC's hand is freezing for an hour.

**Speaks to it:** It turns. Looks at them. Opens its mouth. What comes out is **the PC's own voice** saying the thing they most want to hear:
- Gareth's says: "I know the answer."
- Halpin's says: "They believe."
- Wolfram's says: "You are never alone."
- Ted's says: "It's obvious."
- Filberta's says: "They'll remember this."

Then it's gone.

**Attacks it:** It doesn't flinch. The weapon passes through. It looks disappointed. Then gone.

---

## Aftermath

The Fetches don't return (unless you want them to escalate later). But:

- PCs who saw their Fetch may **dream** of it — briefly, at the edge of sleep. Not a full vision. Just a flash of that stillness, that contentment, that hollowness.
- The Fetch represents what the Faedom is OFFERING each PC — without ever asking directly. The Faedom doesn't tempt. It just shows you what you could be.
- If a PC later ACCEPTS a Fae's boon, remind them: "You saw this. You saw what it looks like from the outside."

## GM Notes

- **This is NOT a combat encounter.** Don't roll initiative. Don't give it stats. It's a mirror.
- **Don't explain what it is.** Let PCs figure out "that was me" on their own. If they ask Fae NPCs about it later, Fae might say: "Ah. You met yourself. Was it kind?"
- **Timing matters.** Deploy AFTER PCs have met enough Fae to have been tempted, but BEFORE anyone accepts a boon. It's a warning label that arrives before the product.
- **Let players be disturbed.** Don't rush past it. Describe slowly. Let the silence after land.
- **Filberta's is the cruelest.** She's a Would-Be Hero — someone who WANTS to be great. Showing her a version that IS great but hollow hits her specific wound. Handle with care based on Kari's comfort level.
- **The Fetch is folklore-accurate.** It's not a Fae creature — it's an omen. It has no agenda. It doesn't serve anyone. It just... shows.
