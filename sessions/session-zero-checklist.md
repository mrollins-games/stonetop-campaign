## Phase 3: How the Game Works (~10 min)

> Cover the unique mechanical concepts so players can make informed playbook choices. Not a full rules teach — just enough to understand their character sheets.

### 3a. Conflict Resolution — The Dice (~2 min)

**[Show slide: Conflict Resolution — 2d6 + Modifier]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "This is a 2d6 system. When you do something risky or uncertain, you roll 2d6 plus one of your six stats — STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, or CHA. Same stats as D&D, but the modifiers range from -1 to +3.
>
> - **10+ = Strong hit** — full success, you nailed it
> - **7-9 = Weak hit** — you do it, but with a cost, complication, or hard choice
> - **6- = Miss** — something bad happens, and you mark XP. Failure is how you grow.
>
> Important: a miss doesn't mean 'you failed at the thing.' It means *I* get to make a move. That might be worse than simple failure.
>
> Two modifiers: **Advantage** means roll 3d6, keep best 2. **Disadvantage** means roll 3d6, keep worst 2. They cancel each other out."

### 3b. Moves (~2 min)

**[Show slide: Moves — categorized table]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "You don't pick from an action menu. You describe what your character does in the fiction — 'I grab the ledge before I fall' or 'I stare him down and demand he talk.' If what you describe triggers a move, we roll. If you're not sure if something is a move, just describe it and I'll tell you.
>
> Moves come in categories:"

- [ ] Present moves table:

| Category | Moves |
|----------|-------|
| **Basic** (everyone) | Aid, Clash, Defend, Defy Danger, Interfere, Know Things, Let Fly, Persuade, Seek Insight |
| **Special** (always in play) | Advantage/Disadvantage, Burn Brightly, End of Session, Death's Door |
| **Expedition** | Chart a Course, Outfit, Requisition, Have What You Need, Recover, Struggle as One, Keep Company, Return Triumphant, Make Camp |
| **Homefront** (village downtime) | Bolster, Convalesce, Deploy, Level Up, Make a Plan, Meet with Disaster, Muster, Pull Together, Seasons Change, Trade & Barter |
| **Follower** | Order Followers, Strengthen Your Bond |
| **Playbook** (class-specific) | Unique to each class — gained at creation and on level-up |

> "Key point: if you're not sure if something triggers a move, just describe what you do. I'll tell you if we need to roll. Some moves don't even require rolls — they just give you choices."

### 3c. Gear & Quantum Inventory (~2 min)

**[Show slide: Gear — Load, Supplies, Undefined Items]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "This one's fun. You don't pre-pack a gear list. When you set out on an expedition, you pick your **load level**:
> - **Light** (3 slots) — quick and quiet
> - **Normal** (4-6 slots)
> - **Heavy** (7-9 slots) — noisy, slow, quick to tire
>
> Items have weight markers (◆ or ◆◆). You mark them as you use them, not before. Here's the key move:
>
> **Have What You Need:** When you decide you had something all along, move a mark from 'undefined' to a specific item. It must be something you *could* have had all along.
>
> So: 'I reach into my pack and pull out rope.' You had rope. Mark a slot. When you're out of slots, you're out of stuff.
>
> **Supplies** are a special item — 4+Prosperity uses per ◆. Spend them to Recover HP, Make Camp, or produce extra small items.
>
> **Provisions** are foraged food — use in place of supplies for camping/travel, but they spoil.
>
> **Small items** fit in a pocket — they don't count against load. You get 4+Prosperity of them when you Outfit."

### 3e. Other Mechanics (~1 min)

**[Show slide: Mechanics — Armor, Hold, HP]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "A few more mechanical concepts:
>
> **Armor & +Armor:** Your armor value subtracts from incoming damage. Regular armor doesn't stack — you use the highest. But '+1 armor' items (like a shield) *do* stack on top.
>
> **Piercing:** Ignores that many points of armor. '*x* piercing' means it equals Stonetop's Prosperity stat.
>
> **Hold:** Some moves say 'hold 3' or 'hold 2 Readiness.' Think of it as a currency specific to that move. You spend it 1-for-1 on options listed in the move. When the situation changes, you lose unspent hold.
>
> **HP & Damage:** Damage is based on your playbook, not your weapon — the Heavy deals d10 regardless of what they swing. When you hit 0 HP, you make a Death's Door roll. Not instant death, but healing is slow — be careful.
>
> **Debilities:** Conditions that give you disadvantage on rolls with a specific stat. They last until you Convalesce."

### 3f. XP & Leveling (~1 min)

**[Show slide: XP — Sources & End of Session]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "You gain XP from several sources:
>
> - **Miss (6-):** Every time you roll a miss, mark XP. Failure teaches.
> - **Being convinced:** When another PC successfully Persuades you and you do what they want, mark XP.
> - **End of Session move:** At the end of every session, we go through these as a group:
>   - Did you demonstrate or struggle with your **instinct**? → mark XP
>   - Did your relationship with a PC, NPC, or group **change**? → mark XP
>   - Did we **learn more** about the world or its history? → everyone marks XP
>   - Did we **defeat a threat** to Stonetop or the region? → everyone marks XP
>   - Did we **improve standing** with neighbors? → everyone marks XP
>   - Did we **make lasting improvement** to Stonetop? → everyone marks XP
>
> **Level Up** when your XP ≥ 6 + (2 × your current level). Subtract that amount, gain a level, pick a new move.
>
> **Burn Brightly:** If you have enough XP to level up, you can spend 2 XP after any roll to add +1. Risk vs. reward — spend XP now, or save it to level?"

### 3g. Appendix: Value & Coins

**[Show slide: Value Scale & Coins — reference only]**

> "Last thing — the economy. No standard currency here. Everything has a **Value** from 0-4, logarithmic scale:"

| Value | Worth roughly... | Coin equivalent |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0 | A favor, common item, a few days labor | Purse of coppers / 1 silver coin |
| 1 | Season of unskilled labor, a unit of trade goods | Handful of silvers |
| 2 | Year of unskilled labor, a cartload of goods, 1 Surplus | Purse of silvers / 1 gold coin |
| 3 | Year of skilled labor, a good horse | Handful of golds |
| 4 | A dozen horses, a "priceless" item | Purse of golds |

> "Coins: individual → handful (~10) → purse (~100). 1 silver ≈ 100 coppers. 1 gold ≈ 100 silvers. But Stonetop runs on trade, favors, and honor — coins are for outsiders.
>
> Don't worry about memorizing this. It's a reference for when trade comes up in play."

### 4a. The Village (~4 min)

**[Show slides: Village map + Standing Stone art]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "Let me paint you a picture of home.
>
> Stonetop sits on a bluff overlooking the Great Wood — a dense, dangerous forest that stretches as far as anyone's gone. The village is built on ancient ruins. Giant-sized foundations, from a forgotten people we call the Makers. Nobody knows who they were.
>
> The most obvious landmark is **the Stone** itself — 50+ feet tall, covered in faded runes no one can read. During storms, lightning strikes it constantly. You've lived here your whole life, so you barely notice. Outsiders are terrified of it.
>
> Other landmarks you'll see on the map:
> - **The Cistern** — ancient Maker-built water storage, still works perfectly
> - **The Old Wall** — a crumbling mile-wide perimeter; villagers quarry it for building stone
> - **The Ringwall** — waist-high fieldstone wall around the village core
> - **The Pavilion of the Gods** — open-air shrines to Helior, Danu, Aratis, and Tor
> - **The Public House** — the social hub, run by the Publican
> - **The Granary** — communal food storage
>
> Life here is simple. You're farmers — barley, oats, beans, potatoes. Some hunters and trappers work the Wood. There's a smith, a tanner, a midwife, a potter — but most people farm. Iron is common, good steel is rare, bronze is old-fashioned but everywhere.
>
> There's no currency here. Everything runs on barter, debt, and honor. Coins are for outsiders."

### 4b. The Neighbors (~4 min)

**[Show slides: Vicinity map + World's End map]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "You're not alone out here, but you're not exactly in good company either. Let me show you the neighborhood.
>
> **The Great Wood** — it's right there, on your doorstep. Hunters and trappers make a living from it, but nobody goes deep without good reason. It's dangerous.
>
> **The Forest Folk** — they lived in the Wood for as long as anyone remembers. Had a compact with Stonetop: we'd never cut a living tree, they'd trade with us. Ten years ago, they vanished. Every single one of them, gone overnight. Nobody knows why.
>
> **The Fae** — magical, fickle beings that dwell in the Wood. The Forest Folk called them 'Good Neighbors' and warned us to be respectful and careful. Without the Folk as intermediaries... things are uncertain.
>
> **Crinwin** — horrid little grey-skinned mimics in the Wood. They imitate voices, steal children. Growing bolder since the Folk left.
>
> Further afield:
> - **Gordin's Delve** — mining town, few days west in the mountains. Rough, lawless place.
> - **The Hillfolk** — nomadic horselords of the Steplands to the south. Fierce, proud. They consider Gordin's Delve corrupt.
> - **Marshedge** — proper town, 10 days south-east. Run by a former bandit lord named Brennan. The closest thing to civilization.
> - **Barrier Pass** — few days north in the mountains. Stoic, unfriendly, don't want visitors. Something's off about them.
>
> That's it. That's the known world from Stonetop's perspective. Beyond that — here be dragons."

### 4c. Dangers & Mysteries (~3 min)

**[Show slide: Mood collage — dark water, cave mouth, red trees, ruins]**

**GM Talk Track:**

> "Now the fun stuff — what's out there that wants to kill you. Or worse.
>
> **Deep water** — everyone in Stonetop knows this. Evil dwells in deep water. Lakes, rivers, anything you can't see the bottom of. Don't go near it. This isn't superstition — people have died.
>
> **The Things Below** — primeval entities of darkness, corruption, and ruin. Ancient beyond imagining. Their power taints everything it touches. They're sealed away... mostly.
>

>

> - **The Pale Hunter** — stalks the Great Wood on certain moonlit nights. A force of nature, not a god. Impress him and you're rewarded. Get caught by him... don't think about it.
> - **The Maw** — a cave north of town. No underbrush grows near its entrance. Bones litter the ground. Strange noises at night. Nobody goes there.
> - **The Red Grove** — a stand of trees that turn blood red in autumn, weeks before anything else changes. Back to normal by spring. The Forest Folk avoided it.
> - **Earthquakes** — getting more frequent. The old stories speak of the Crombil, a fiery serpent of woe. Maybe it stirs. Or maybe something worse.
> - **The Ruined Tower** — a day's march away. Giant-sized tunnels beneath it. Generations ago, people went deep and brought back amazing things. Nobody's tried in a long time.
>
