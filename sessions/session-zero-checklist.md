## Phase 1: Present the Setting (~12 min)

> Present these highlights verbally with the slide deck. Don't ask players to read the handout — walk them through it.

### 1a. The Premise (~2 min)

> "You play the heroes of **Stonetop**, an isolated village near the edge of the known world. Your adventures focus on threats to the village, opportunities for the village, or personal goals. Months or years can pass between adventures."

- [ ] Hit key points:
  - ~300 people, tight-knit, poor but proud
  - Everyone pulls their weight — community IS the strength
  - You have deep ties here. You're not rootless adventurers — you're *their* heroes
  - The world is dangerous and mostly unknown beyond a few days' travel

### 1b. The Village (~3 min)

> "Stonetop sits on a bluff overlooking the Great Wood. It's built on ancient ruins — giant-sized, from a forgotten people called the Makers."

- [ ] Key landmarks:
  - **The Stone** — 50+ feet tall, covered in faded runes, older than anything else. Lightning strikes it constantly during storms. Locals barely notice.
  - **The Cistern** — ancient Maker-built water storage
  - **The Old Wall** — crumbling mile-wide perimeter, villagers mine it for building stone
  - **The Ringwall** — waist-high fieldstone wall around the village core
  - **The Pavilion of the Gods** — shrines to Helior, Danu, Aratis, and Tor
  - **The Public House** — social hub, floor space for travelers
  - **The Granary** — communal food storage
- [ ] Economy & life:
  - Farmers (barley, oats, beans, potatoes), hunters, trappers
  - Only a few full-time trades: smith, tanner, midwife, publican, potter
  - Iron is common. Good steel is rare. Bronze is old-fashioned but everywhere
  - Barter, debt, and honor — no coins circulating locally

### 1c. The Gods (~1 min)

> "Four gods have shrines in the Pavilion. None of you have to worship any of them, but they're part of daily life."

- [ ] Present:
  - **Tor** — Rain-Maker, Thunderhead, Slayer-of-Beasts. Most popular. Associated with the Stone itself.
  - **Helior** — The Daybringer. God of sun, light, hope, mercy.
  - **Danu** — The Earth Mother. Nature, fertility, the wild.
  - **Aratis** — The Lawkeeper. Civilization, order, community, truth.

### 1d. The Neighbors (~2 min)

> "You're not alone out here, but you're not exactly in good company either."

- [ ] Present:
  - **The Great Wood** — dangerous forest on your doorstep. Hunters and trappers ply it for fur and meat.
  - **The Forest Folk** — disappeared 10 years ago. No one knows why. Had a compact with Stonetop: never cut a living tree.
  - **The Fae** — magical, fickle beings in the Wood. The Folk called them "Good Neighbors" and warned Stonetop to be careful.
  - **Crinwin** — horrid little grey-skinned mimics in the Wood. Growing bolder since the Folk left.
  - **Gordin's Delve** — mining town, few days west in mountains. Rough place.
  - **The Hillfolk** — nomadic horselords of the Steplands. Fierce, proud, consider Gordin's Delve corrupt.
  - **Marshedge** — proper town, 10 days south-east. Run by a former bandit named Brennan.
  - **Barrier Pass** — few days north in mountains. Stoic, unfriendly, don't want visitors.

### 1e. Dangers & Mysteries (~2 min)

> "The world is full of things that will kill you. Here's what everyone in town knows to fear."

- [ ] Present:
  - **Deep water** — evil dwells there. Everyone knows it. Don't go near lakes or rivers.
  - **The Things Below** — primeval entities of darkness, corruption, ruin. Their power taints everything it touches.
  - **Magic** — not common, not easy, not safe. Comes from spirits, gods, Things Below, or Maker lore.
  - **The Pale Hunter** — stalks the Wood on certain nights. Force of nature, not a god. Impress him = rewarded. Get caught = don't think about it.
  - **The Maw** — a cave north of town. No underbrush grows near it. Bones litter the entrance. Strange noises at night.
  - **The Red Grove** — trees turn blood red in autumn, back to normal by spring. Forest Folk avoided it.
  - **Earthquakes** — getting more frequent. Maybe the Crombil (fiery serpent of woe) stirs. Or something worse.
  - **The Ruined Tower** — day's march away. Giant-sized tunnels beneath. People who went deep brought back amazing things, generations ago.

### 1f. Who You Are (~1 min)

> "You are a local hero. You have deep ties to Stonetop. You might be..."

- [ ] Read one-line descriptions for available playbooks:
  - **The Judge** — settler of disputes, chronicler, divine bulwark against chaos
  - **The Seeker** — collector of lost lore and power, with potent artifacts
  - **The Lightbearer** — invokes divine power via flame. Beacon of hope, foe of the dark
  - **The Blessed** — nature priest, speaks to spirits, works subtle magic _(if James considers this)_
  - **The Heavy** — our violent individual. Champion, but a bit of a liability _(if James considers this)_

### 1g. Your Agenda & Principles (~1 min)

> "As players, your goals are simple:"

- [ ] Present:
  - **Portray a compelling character**
  - **Engage with the fictional world**
  - **Play to find out what happens**
- [ ] Highlight 2-3 principles:
  - Be bold, take risks — if you don't act like a hero, who will?
  - Embrace difficulty and failure — show us how your character deals with it
  - Participate in worldbuilding — YOUR answers define this world

> "My agenda as GM: portray a rich world, punctuate your lives with adventure, play to find out what happens. I don't have a pre-written plot. We discover the story together."

### 1h. Tone (~30 sec)

> "Stonetop takes itself seriously. Violence has consequences. Monsters are scary. Magic is strange and dangerous. People matter. We'll have moments of levity — this is for fun — but it works best played mostly straight."
