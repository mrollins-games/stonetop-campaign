# The Hunters' Route — Filberta's Handout

*For Kari — what Filberta grew up knowing about D8, E7, and E5.*

---

## The Rooted Pass (D8)

A steep hillside, almost a cliff, choked with ancient roots — gnarled, interlocking, black when wet. The gaps between them drop into unseen tangles. There is no obvious path. But there IS a path, if you grew up climbing it.

Third root from the split oak — that one holds. The mossy one below it is rotten; skip it. Place your foot where the two thick roots cross — there's a ledge. The flat grey root that looks stable? Hollow. Step on the edge, not the centre.

Your father dragged you up and down this pass starting when you were seven. You hated it. You learned it anyway. Now you could do it blindfolded.

At the base, the forest opens into massive old-growth trunks spaced wider. Game trails cut everywhere. This is where the hunting begins.

## The Food Nexus (E7)

The forest here is different. Oaks, beeches, hazel — growing in layers, spaced too evenly. The ground is carpeted in acorn caps, beech husks, hazelnut shells. Game trails converge from every direction. Boar root contentedly. Deer tracks everywhere.

You've foraged here since you were a child. The berries are always plentiful. The mushroom logs are always productive. Wild garlic carpets the ground in spring. You told your father once that the trees seemed planted on purpose. He laughed. "Don't overthink it, girl. Just be grateful."

You were right.

### Why no one else comes here

You've thought about this. The hunting here is the best in the Great Wood — better than anywhere within a day's walk of Stonetop. But the route stays quiet. Three, maybe four people use it regularly. Why?

**Hunters don't share.** It's part of the code. You don't point newcomers at your grounds. "Game's good in the deep wood" — that's all anyone says. The WHERE stays with those who earned it.

**The squirrels keep it that way.** Anyone who doesn't already know the route gets spotted by the relay. The alarm cascades. Game disperses before they're within bowshot. They come once, catch nothing, and don't come back. The forest self-selects for the patient. For the quiet. For *you.*

**There's nothing to describe.** You've tried — you've tried to tell Halpin about this place more than once. But the words go sideways every time. You end up talking about the squirrels. About the way the light moves. About how it FEELS. The place itself — what it IS, what it gives — doesn't survive translation. "Big trees. Good game. Go past the steep bit." Useless. He nods politely. He doesn't understand. No one does until they stand in it.

**Your father guards it.** He tells people there's plenty of game closer to home. "No need to push that deep." He's possessive of this stretch of forest in a way he's possessive of nothing else — not his camp, not his daughter. THIS is what he protects.

## The Squirrel Colony (E7)

### How you met them

You were nine. Your father left you at the edge of the coppice — "Wait here, I'll be back by sundown" — and walked north along the trail. Within the hour, the canopy above you erupted. Red-furred bodies, dozens of them, pouring through the branches. Chattering, leaping, chasing each other in spirals around the trunks.

They froze when they saw you. You froze too.

Then one — smaller than the rest, bold — dropped to a low branch and stared at you. You held out a hazelnut from your pocket. It took it. It came back. It brought a friend.

By the end of that day you were sitting in a circle of eight squirrels, sharing your lunch, talking to them like they were people. When your father's footsteps sounded on the trail coming back, they vanished. All of them. Instantly. Like they'd never been there.

### What you figured out

They're never there when your father is. NEVER. Not once in all the years you've come here. You figured it out eventually:

They know where he is. They always know. The colony communicates in relay — alarm calls passed tree-to-tree, directional, specific. Not just "danger" but "danger THERE, moving THIS direction." One squirrel spots him entering D8, calls to the next, calls to the next. By the time he reaches E7, every squirrel is elsewhere.

They've been doing this for generations. Your father has never once successfully trapped a squirrel in this hex. He has no idea why.

### Your squirrel

One of them left the colony to follow you. It chose you. It rode your shoulder home to Stonetop and never went back. The colony remembers it — and when it returns, they recognise it immediately. They cluster around it. Calm. Welcoming. This is home.

It doesn't go manic here. It settles. It sits on a familiar branch and chatters softly with the others. It's catching up.

## The Cusp (E7, NE edge)

### How you found it

You were eleven. Your father left you in E7 as usual. You waited for the squirrels. They didn't come. (Your father was too close — you know that now.) You waited. Waited. Got bored.

You walked further NE than you'd ever gone. The managed trees thinned. The undergrowth changed — wilder, older. And then you saw them.

Jade-green crystalline formations. Waist-high, translucent, shaped like crashing waves caught mid-break — spray frozen as solid arcs. They ran in a line as far as you could see, curving away into the trees in both directions.

You touched one. Cool. Smooth. Faintly humming.

You knew — in the way children know things without being told — that this was a border. The other side was different. You crossed it anyway.

## The Welcome Glade (E5)

A lightning-bolt-shaped clearing. Widest where it meets the river, narrowing in sharp zig-zag angles as it cuts north into the trees. Knee-high grass, wildflowers, sunlight. The river is audible to the SW.

Your father's camp is in the narrow northern bend — a hazel bower he's used for years. Living poles bent into a dome, oiled hide over the frame. It's semi-permanent. Comfortable. His.

From here the trail leads north into E7. He is never there when the animal dies. He is never there in the broader sense — gone before dawn, back after dark, always somewhere else when it matters.

### The river crossing

The ford between here and D4. Chest-high in summer, neck-high in spring. Never calm — sometimes calmer. The current is vicious; what looks like gentle surface flow hides an undertow that drags at your legs mid-stream. The riverbed is uneven — smooth stones give way to thigh-deep holes. Algae-slick rocks shift underfoot. Most townsfolk won't attempt it. You've crossed it many times. It's always felt... manageable. More manageable than it should. You can't explain why.

## The Offering (what hunters know)

When you make a kill, you leave an offering. Every hunter knows this. It's the first thing you're taught — or the first thing you learn from watching. You leave something: a portion of the meat, arranged a certain way, left at the kill site. You don't ask what it's for. You just do it.

The legend among hunters: if someone who doesn't know the offering were to hunt, they'd be thinned from the herd. This is why non-hunters don't hunt. It's not a rule anyone enforces — it enforces itself.

Your father traps instead of hunts. The animals die when he isn't there. He thinks this means he doesn't have to make the offering. He thinks he's clever.

You think he's a coward.
