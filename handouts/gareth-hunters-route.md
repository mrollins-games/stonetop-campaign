# The Source Upstream — Gareth's Handout (DRAFT)

**For:** Danny R. (Gareth)  
**Represents:** What Gareth knows about the ley line flowing into Stonetop and his attempts to trace it to its source  
**Give when:** Before the expedition into the Great Wood begins  
**Status:** DRAFT — resolution options at the end need GM decision

---

## Context (GM only — do not include in player copy)

*Gareth's relationship to the hunting grounds is unique: he doesn't care about the game, the forest, or the spirits. He cares about the ENERGY. The Azure Hand has sensed a current flowing into Stonetop from the south-east since the day he arrived. He's been obsessed with finding its source. The river has stopped him — but not for the reasons he thinks.*

*He interprets his failed crossings as ley line resistance — the energy flowing toward him creates "headwinds" that the Azure Hand amplifies. In reality, the drowned girl spirit is ALSO pushing him back (same as everyone). He hasn't discovered her. From his perspective, it's purely an energy phenomenon.*

---

## The Current

You noticed it the first week. Standing in your laboratory, Azure Hand humming against your palm, and there it was — a flow. Faint but constant. Coming from the south-east, passing through Stonetop, continuing north-west. Like standing in a river you can't see.

Every energy source you'd studied before had been local. A battery. A reservoir. A node. But THIS was a current — moving, continuous, coming from somewhere and going somewhere. Not stored. Not static. *Flowing.*

You spent three months mapping it from within Stonetop. Walking the village at night, staff extended, feeling where the current ran strongest. It follows no road, no building line, no logical path — it cuts through the earth on its own trajectory. South-east to north-west. Always the same direction. Always the same strength. Patient. Ancient. Indifferent to the village built on top of it.

> The Hand prickles when you face into the current. Not pain — excitement. The etherium plate vibrates at a frequency you feel in your teeth. It WANTS to drink from this. It wants to gather. You don't let it. Not yet. Not until you understand what you're touching.

The current's origin is south-east. Beyond the bluff. Beyond the river.

You went to find it.

---

## The River

The cliff edge gives you the clearest reading. Stand at the bluff with the Hand extended toward the forest — the current intensifies. It's coming from across the river. Specifically: from that bright gap in the canopy, the lightning-bolt glade visible from here. The energy pours out of that opening like breath from a mouth and crosses the water without slowing.

The river doesn't interrupt it. Water doesn't block it. The current passes through the river as if the river weren't there.

But YOU can't.

---

## The Attempts

### First Attempt (spring, year one)

You went to the ford at dawn. The one everyone says is impassable — chest-high water, vicious undertow, algae-slick stones. You'd studied the readings: the ley line crosses here, flowing east-to-west. You'd be swimming INTO the current. Against it.

You reasoned: the current is energy, not physical force. It shouldn't affect a body. The Hand might tingle, but you'd push through.

You were wrong.

The moment you waded past your waist, the Hand began to PULL. Not toward the far bank — toward you. The current flowing into the staff created resistance — like walking into a gale while holding a sail. Every step deeper meant more surface area of your body intersecting the flow. The etherium plate buzzed so loudly you could hear it over the water.

At chest height, something pushed you back. Not the physical current — though that was bad enough — but a pressure in the energy itself. A wall. A resistance. As if the ley line OBJECTED to something moving against its flow.

Your feet slipped. The undertow grabbed. You went under — swallowed water, tumbled, smashed your knee on a rock — and washed up on the NEAR bank, coughing. Twenty feet downstream from where you'd entered.

The Hand was still buzzing. Still drinking. The current hadn't changed at all. It didn't care that you'd tried.

### Second Attempt (summer, year one)

Calmer conditions. Lower water. You wrapped the Hand in oiled leather — theory: reduce its interaction with the current, reduce the resistance. You'd tested this in your lab: shielding the etherium dampened its sensitivity.

It worked — partially. The pull was less. You made it further. Waist. Chest. The current shoved but you braced and pushed. Your footing held. You were ALMOST to the midpoint —

And then the resistance doubled. Tripled. Like hitting an invisible wall. Not gradual — sudden. One step: manageable. Next step: impossible. The energy wasn't flowing past you anymore; it was flowing THROUGH you, and your body was rejecting it the way iron rejects a magnet of the same polarity.

You tried to push through. You couldn't. It wasn't strength — it was physics. Your body refused to move forward. The current deposited you back on the near bank like flotsam.

You sat on the rocks, shivering, and wrote six pages of notes.

### Third Attempt (autumn, year one)

Different approach. You went at night. Theory: ley lines might pulse with solar cycles — weaker in darkness, stronger in light. If there was a nocturnal lull —

There wasn't. The current was identical. The Hand still buzzed. The resistance hit at the same point. You didn't even make it as far this time — exhausted from two previous attempts, cold, frustrated. The river spat you back.

Three attempts. Three failures. Same result every time: the ley line won't let you cross against its flow. Or more precisely: the Azure Hand won't let you cross against its flow. The interaction between the staff's etherium and the current creates a physical resistance that scales with proximity to the current's centre.

You've been studying it from the near bank ever since. Pointing. Measuring. Theorising. The source is THERE — in that glade, or beyond it, deeper into the forest. You can trace it. You can FEEL it. You just can't reach it.

## What You've Mapped (from the D4 bank)

Months of observation. Notebooks full of readings. Here's what you know:

- **Direction:** The current flows from deep in the forest (NE, roughly) through the glade, across the river, through D4, through Stonetop, and continues NW. One line. Unwavering.
- **Strength:** Moderate at the river. Stronger at the glade (you can tell from the readings — the intensity at the source is higher than what arrives at Stonetop). It weakens over distance. Something feeds it from the NE.
- **Depth:** The current runs underground — maybe thirty feet down in most places. At the glade it surfaces (the gap in the canopy IS the ley line's surface expression — trees can't grow in its direct path). At the river it's shallow — maybe ten feet under the riverbed.
- **Pulse:** None detected. Constant flow. No tidal variation, no diurnal cycle, no seasonal change. Whatever powers it, powers it always.
- **Age:** Old. The fact that trees have grown AROUND it (leaning away from the glade's edges) means it's been flowing for centuries minimum. Possibly millennia.
- **The glade's shape:** Lightning bolt. The ley line carved that shape — burned absence into the forest over deep time. This is what a ley line looks like on the surface when it's strong enough and old enough: a scar where nothing grows.

**What you DON'T know (and want to):**
- Where does it originate? Somewhere NE, deeper in the forest. How deep? What's the source?
- Why does it flow to Stonetop? Coincidence? Or was Stonetop built ON the current deliberately?
- What feeds it? A source this constant should have a reservoir or generator.
- Is the current weakening? You think so — readings over 18 months suggest a very slow decline. Barely perceptible. But real.
- Can you TAP it? The Hand WANTS to gather from this. You haven't let it. Yet.

## GM DECISION NEEDED — How Does Gareth Eventually Cross?

*Three options. Pick one (or modify) before finalising this handout:*

### Option A: He Never Has

Gareth has never crossed the river. The expedition is his FIRST time reaching E5. His knowledge is entirely from the D4 bank — theoretical, observational, obsessive. He's been studying this current for over a year without physically reaching it.

**Narrative payoff:** The expedition gives him what he's wanted since he arrived. His excitement is volcanic. He'll be tracking the ley line the entire journey, vibrating with anticipation. Also — when the party crosses the river together and the drowned girl helps them, Gareth will feel the "resistance" ease and misinterpret WHY (he'll think group mass distributes the headwind; really, the spirit let them through because Wolfram asked).

**Mechanical note:** He has extremely detailed theoretical knowledge but zero ground-truth. His first readings from E5 will confirm or overturn months of models.

---

### Option B: He Built a Raft

Gareth reasoned: the resistance scales with SUBMERSION in the current's flow plane. A body in the water intersects more of the current than a body ABOVE it. Solution: don't swim. Float on top.

He built a crude raft — logs, rope, a pole. Launched it from the near bank. The ley line resistance was still there but dramatically reduced — the raft sits above the water, above the current's densest layer. He made it across once. Barely. The raft nearly capsized twice. He spent four hours on the far bank taking readings before the return crossing (WITH the current — trivially easy) brought him home.

**Narrative payoff:** He's been to E5 exactly once. He has ground-truth readings from the glade. He knows the current is stronger there. But he didn't explore beyond the glade — four hours wasn't enough, and he was alone. He wants to go DEEPER. The expedition gives him time and companions.

**Mechanical note:** He has confirmed readings from E5 (the glade's energy signature, the surface expression, the concentration at the northern tip). He does NOT have readings from E7 or beyond.

---

### Option C: He Followed the Ley Line Overland (via D8)

Gareth couldn't cross the river. So he went around. He followed the current's projected path overland — south from Stonetop, around the river's bend, and approached the forest from the west/south. He found the Rooted Pass at D8 and descended partway — far enough to confirm the ley line runs through there too (the current beneath his feet, running south from somewhere further NE). But alone, in unfamiliar forest, without hunter knowledge of the terrain — he turned back before reaching E7.

**Narrative payoff:** He knows D8 has the ley line too. He can confirm the current runs THROUGH the pass and into the deep wood. But he doesn't know E7 or E5 from ground level. The expedition completes his map — fills in the middle section he couldn't reach alone.

**Mechanical note:** He has a partial overland reading (Stonetop → D8) that he's never been able to connect to the river reading (E5 → D4). The expedition closes the circuit. When the full path becomes clear (C9→D8→E7→E5→D4→Stonetop), it'll be a revelation.

- The ley line originates at C9 (Forest Folk outpost) — he can't know this yet.
- The current originally powered a purification cistern and cultivation system. The Forest Folk built it.

- The glade's ley line is the same current that feeds the Food Nexus root network in E7.
