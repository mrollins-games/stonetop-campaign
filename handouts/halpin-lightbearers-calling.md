## The Vision (Age 4–5)

You stared into the sun. Children are told not to. You did it anyway — not once, not by accident, but deliberately, day after day, drawn to it the way some children are drawn to water.

Your sight went white. Then it went dark.

> In the darkness, warmth. A hand — not a hand, but the idea of a hand — touched your face. A voice that was also light spoke truths you cannot quite remember. You felt seen. You felt chosen. You felt safe.

When your sight returned (days later? weeks?), the world looked different. Light had weight. Shadows had texture. And fire listened to you.

## The Shrine in the Pavilion

Your mother Dailin keeps it. She polishes the bronze sun-disc, replaces the candles, sweeps the alcove. She does this every morning at dawn and tells anyone who'll listen about it.

> A stone alcove in the Pavilion of the Gods. A bronze disc — hammered, not cast — hangs on the back wall. Below it, a stone shelf holds candles (yours — beeswax, pale gold, sweet-smelling). In the mornings the disc catches the first light through the eastern gap in the wall and throws a bright oval onto the floor.

The shrine has been well-tended and given due respect. Helior does not hold the highest place — that's Tor, by tradition — but no one neglects it either.

You don't need the shrine. Helior is everywhere the light falls. But people need a place to come TO, and so the shrine exists for them.

## The Apiary

Near the fields, south-facing. Six hives in summer, four through winter. The bees know you — they land on your bare arms and never sting. (They sting your mother. Frequently.)

> The smell of warm wax and clover. The low hum that changes pitch with the weather. Smoke curling from the bellows. Honey — golden, thick, medicinal.

The honey goes to the village (cooking, wound-dressing, trade). The wax becomes candles — your candles, which burn in every home that wants them and always in the shrine.

---

## The Distillery

Copper tubes, clay vessels, a stone hearth. You make three things:

- **Mead** — honey-wine, golden, sweet. For celebrations. For comfort.
- **Brandy** — fruit spirits, sharp, warming. For the cold months.
- **Whisky** — grain spirits, aged in small casks. "Helior's trapped sunlight" — you save this for midwinter, when the days are shortest and people need reminding that warmth exists.

The distillery is yours. You built it. The copper came from Gordin's Delve in trade. The village benefits — surplus from mead trade was noted in the Seasons Change.

## Your Powers

### Consecrated Flame
You whisper to a flame and it becomes holy. The light it casts is uncomfortable — not to people, but to things that shouldn't be here. Creatures of darkness flinch from it. It lasts until the flame dies or you consecrate another.

### Invoke the Sun God
You pour Helior's power into a holy light. It's not free — there's always a cost. The light demands something.

> *You've done this once in the field (Session 1 — healing Cefin). The consequence was absolute: every flame in the Birds & Bees died. Not extinguished — VOIDED. Anti-fire. The hearth still won't relight.*

## What You Believe

Every sunrise is a new day and a new chance to do good. This isn't optimism — it's theology. The sun doesn't judge yesterday. It returns regardless. It offers warmth to everyone equally. You try to do the same.

You never disparage anyone. Every mistake is a learning opportunity. When people sit in your house and talk, you listen, and when they leave they feel better. You don't do anything — you just pay attention. (That IS the move. Radiant.)

## Stories

### The Healing at the Birds & Bees

Spring had barely arrived. Cefin stumbled in wounded — axe-cut, bleeding, something wrong behind his eyes. You placed your hands on him and called the light.

It came. Oh, it came. A 12 — two sixes. The wound closed. Cefin breathed. And then every flame in the room died. Not flickered — ceased. The hearth went cold and STAYED cold. Something spoke through Cefin in a language only Wolfram understood.

The cost of miracles.

### Dailin Tries to Stop You

Your mother stood in the doorway. "No, Halpin, don't risk yourself!" She meant it. She always means it. She also always makes it about her — about what losing you would mean for HER, for the shrine, for her position.

You went anyway. You always go.
