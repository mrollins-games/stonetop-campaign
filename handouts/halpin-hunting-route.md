# The Hunting Route — Halpin's Handout

**For:** Dan G. (Halpin)  
**Represents:** What Halpin knows about D8, E7, and E5 as a lifelong Stonetop resident — not a hunter, but someone who listens when people talk by his fire.  
**Give when:** First expedition south into the Great Wood, or when the party discusses the hunting route.

---

## Context (GM only — do not include in player copy)

*Halpin is NOT a hunter. He's never walked this route himself. But he's the guy everyone talks to — at night, in his house, by candlelight. Hunters drink his mead and tell stories. Filberta crashes on his floor and talks about the forest. He's absorbed a surprising amount secondhand.*

*What he DOES have that nobody else does: a spiritual connection to Helior that will manifest physically in these hexes (the bees, the warmth, the Sunlit Hollow). He doesn't know about the Hollow yet — that's discovery. But he knows his bees behave strangely when he faces south-east, and he's always wondered why.*

*This handout gives him enough to be useful without stepping on Filberta's expertise.*

---

## What Everyone Knows

The hunting route runs south from Stonetop: down the bluff, across the river at the ford, then D8 → E7 → E5. Hunters have used it for generations. Non-hunters don't go — not because anyone forbids it, but because... you just don't. It's not your place.

You've never gone. You're not a hunter. The forest is beautiful from the clifftop — dark green canopy broken by that one bright patch to the south-east (the glade, E5) — but it's never called to you the way the sunrise does.

Until recently.

---

## The Rooted Pass (D8)

You've heard about it. Everyone who's been down there mentions it — a steep hillside of interlocking roots, slippery in the wet, treacherous if you don't know the path. Hunters describe it casually. "The roots," they say, like it's nothing. But you've seen people come back with torn packs and twisted ankles from misjudging it.

> You know it's steep. You know it's slippery. You know Filberta could walk it blindfolded and you'd need a guide.

That's the extent of it.

---

## The Hunting Grounds (E7)

Hunters come back from this hex fat and happy. "The forest provides," they say — and they mean it. Good foraging, good game trails, abundant mast. Filberta brings back berries, mushrooms, herbs from here every autumn.

You know:
- The trees are spaced strangely — Filberta mentioned it once, late at night. "Too even. Like someone planted them." You didn't press her on it.
- Game converges here. Boar, deer, squirrels (though Dilwen can never seem to trap the squirrels).
- Filberta's squirrel comes from here. The colony she visits is somewhere in this hex.

### The Bees

This is the thing you can't explain.

Your bees — the apiary bees, domestic, predictable — sometimes face south-east on warm days. All of them. Simultaneously. For a few minutes, the whole hive orients the same direction, humming a slightly different note. Then it passes and they go back to foraging.

Once, a single wild bee appeared at your apiary. Not one of yours — different colouring, slightly larger, with a faint warmth to it you could feel when it landed on your hand. It stayed for a minute. Then it flew south-east, toward the forest.

You've never followed it. You've always wondered.

---

## The Welcome Glade (E5)

From the clifftop at Stonetop, looking south-east: a lightning-bolt shaped gap in the forest canopy, bright green against the dark woodland. Everyone knows it's there. Most people have never been.

You know:
- The river crossing is there — the ford between D4 and E5. Chest-high in summer, dangerous in spring.
- People say the crossing is "surprisingly safe" — something about it being manageable. Hunters cross it regularly and shrug.
- Dilwen camps in the narrow northern end. His hazel bower — semi-permanent, been there for years.
- From your house at night, looking south, you can sometimes see the faint pale glow of the glade on moonlit nights — the open grass reflecting light where the forest swallows it everywhere else.

### The Crossing

You've been warned about it since childhood. The river is fast, the undertow vicious, the footing treacherous. Children are told: don't try it.

But then you hear hunters talk about crossing it like it's nothing. "Just pick the right day, find the shallows." They make it sound easy. Too easy, for how dangerous it's supposed to be.

> *You don't know why the contradiction exists. You've never resolved it. It just... is.*

---

## The Offering (what you've overheard)

You are not a hunter. You were never taught the offering. But you are the person people talk to when they've had too much mead by your fire.

You've caught fragments:
- Hunters leave something at the kill site. Always. Every time.
- It's the first thing you learn — or you don't hunt at all.
- The tradition is old. Older than memory. No one questions it.
- Something about consequence. Something about being "thinned from the herd" if you don't know the rule.

You've never asked directly. It feels like asking would cross a line — like knowing without being initiated would be... dangerous? Or at least unwelcome.

## What Helior Tells You

Not in words. Never in words. But in warmth, in direction, in the behaviour of flame and light:

- The south-east means something. You feel it on solstice mornings — the sun rises over that direction and lingers in your awareness longer than it should.
- Your bees know something you don't. They orient toward E7 like compass needles.
- Fire behaves differently when you face that direction. Your consecrated flame burns steadier, warmer. This isn't something you've tested — it's something you've noticed without meaning to.

You don't know what's out there. But Helior does. And he's... pleased? Whatever lives in that forest, in the direction of E7, Helior approves of it. You've felt that your whole life without ever putting it into words.

## Stories

### Filberta on the Floor

She came in late — past midnight, smelling of Klas's ale and wet forest. Kicked off her boots, dropped onto the rug by the fire, and said without preamble:

"The trees in E7 are planted. Planted on purpose. Da laughed at me when I said it, but I'm RIGHT."

You said nothing. You handed her a blanket. She was asleep in minutes.

You've thought about it since. Trees planted on purpose. By whom?

### The Wild Bee

Midsummer, three years ago. You were smoking a hive — routine, gentle, your bees calm as always. And there it was: a bee that wasn't yours. Larger. Deeper gold. It landed on your bare forearm and you felt HEAT — not a sting, but warmth radiating from its body like a tiny coal.

It stayed for perhaps ten breaths. Then it lifted and flew south-east — straight, purposeful, not foraging. Gone into the treeline.

You watched it go. You almost followed. You didn't.

## What They Don't Know (GM only — do not include in player copy)

- The Sunlit Hollow exists in E7 — a sacred site where Helior and Danu converge. Wild bees with luminescent honey. The bee he saw was a scout FROM there.
- The bees in E7 will greet his Consecrated Flame like a friend — they orbit it gently. They know what he is.
- The crossing at E5 is guided by a drowned girl's spirit (not Helior — a mortal ghost). Halpin's sense of contradiction is correct but misattributed.
- The ley line that powers E7's abundance also flows through E5 (the glade IS the ley line's surface path). This isn't Helior — it's older. But Helior approves of what grows from it.
- The Rooted Pass (D8) has a ley line running through it too — Halpin would feel warmth in the soil there, but faintly.
- Halpin's "pull" toward the south-east is Helior nudging him toward the Sunlit Hollow — a pilgrimage he hasn't made yet.
