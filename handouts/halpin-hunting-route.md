# Honey Brandy and Hearsay — Halpin's Handout

**For:** Dan G. (Halpin)  
**Represents:** What Halpin knows about the hunting grounds — all secondhand, all gathered over the lip of a cup in the public house  
**Give when:** After the expedition into the Great Wood already began! Sorry these areas were under developed when we started sesson 3 and the shared fiction we developed means you should have known this earlier. Lets apply this retroactively if thats okay.

## The Cliff

Late afternoon. You're carrying two clay jugs of honey brandy down the path from your workshop — this batch came out well, golden and smooth, the bees were generous this season. The public house needs restocking.

You pass the cliff edge on the way down. You always pass it. You always look.

South-east. The Great Wood spreading out below the bluff in unbroken dark green — except for THAT. The bright gap. The lightning-bolt shape in the canopy where sunlight floods through and the grass grows vivid against the dark forest walls. You can see it from here. You've always been able to see it. On clear days the hazel at the far tip catches the light and throws it back — yellow-green sparks against the shadow.

Beautiful. Open to the sky. Open to Helior's gaze in a way nothing else in that forest is.

You stand there a moment, jugs on your hips, feeling the familiar tug. The wanting-to-see. Then you look down — the river between here and there, fast and white where it breaks over rocks, the far bank impossibly far.

"Well," you say to no one. "I'll never make it over there."

River crossing. Not your domain. Helior bless.

You shift the jugs and walk on toward the pub.

## The Public House

The evening crowd is thin tonight — rain keeping people close to home. You set the jugs behind the bar and Brennan grunts approval, already reaching for cups. "That the dark batch or the light?" "Light." "Good. Mared's been asking."

You settle into your usual corner — not behind the bar, but near it. Close enough to pour refills. Close enough to hear everything.

This is where Stonetop talks. Not at the Stone, not at the well, not in the fields. HERE. When the work is done and the cups are full and the fire is low, people say things they'd never say in daylight.

You listen. You've always listened.

## The Vignettes

### Dilwen, Three Cups Deep

He doesn't come in often. When he does, he drinks alone and leaves early. But tonight he's on his third cup of your brandy and the rain is picking up and he's not going anywhere.

You don't ask him anything. You never do. You just refill his cup and say something about the weather. He grunts.

Then, unprompted: "Good season. Pelts are thick this year. Fox, marten." He stares into his cup. "Deep wood provides."

You wait.

"That pass'll kill a man who doesn't know it, though. Roots are slick this time of year." He drinks. "Broke my ankle on it — oh, twenty years back. Had to crawl home. Learned which ones hold." He taps the bartop with one thick finger. "Third from the split oak. Remember that."

He looks at you. Seems surprised he said it. Finishes his cup, stands, drops a coin. "Don't go down there." And he's gone.

You don't know why he told you. Maybe the brandy. Maybe something else. But you file it away: *third root from the split oak.*

---

### Filberta, Past Midnight

She does this — shows up after everyone else has left, kicks off her muddy boots, and talks AT you while you bank the fire. She doesn't drink much. She just needs an audience.

Tonight she's wired. Eyes bright. Squirrel asleep in her hood.

"The trees are PLANTED, Halpin. I know I've said it before but I'm not wrong. The spacing — oaks here, beeches there, hazel underneath — it's TOO PERFECT. No forest does that on its own." She's pacing. "Da laughed at me. 'Don't overthink it, girl.' But I'm RIGHT."

You hand her a blanket. She doesn't take it.

"And the game! It's not just good, it's — it CONVERGES. Like the trails all lead to one place. Boar, deer, everything. Coming together in the centre like they're being INVITED."

"Invited by what?" you ask.

She stops. Thinks. Opens her mouth. Closes it.

"I don't... I can't..." She makes a frustrated sound. "It's like trying to describe the colour of water. It's just — you'd have to be there." She takes the blanket. Wraps it around her shoulders. "You'd have to stand in it."

She's asleep on the floor in minutes. You cover the fire. You think: *trees planted on purpose. Game invited. By what?*

---

### Old Hywel, Celebrating

Hywel came back from a good run — three deer in two days, the kind of haul that makes a man generous. He's buying rounds for the house. His face is red and happy. His voice carries.

"...and the GLADE, lads — the glade at the end! Open as your mother's arms. Light pouring in. I swear the grass itself is softer there." He gestures expansively with his cup. "A man could sleep there and wake up ten years younger."

Someone laughs. "You say that every time."

"Because it's TRUE every time! There's something about that place — the air, the light, I don't know. Makes you want to STAY." He drinks. Wipes his mouth. "But the crossing home — hah. Don't try it drunk. Current's a bastard."

You lean in. "Is it as bad as they say? The ford?"

Hywel looks at you. Shrugs. "Funny thing. It LOOKS murderous. And it IS bad — make no mistake, you'll swallow water. But..." He squints, searching for words. "I've never lost anyone there. Not once. Not in thirty years of crossings. And some of us have been STUPID drunk trying it." He laughs. "Maybe Helior's looking out for fools."

"Helior bless," you say automatically.

"Helior bless," he echoes, and lifts his cup.

You think: *thirty years, no drownings, and no one questions it.*

---

### Brennan the Publican, Wiping Down

The crowd's gone. Just you and Brennan and the embers. He's wiping the bar with his rag — slow circles, the way he does when he's thinking.

"My grandfather used to say something," he offers. Doesn't look up. "About the offering."

You go still. You've heard fragments of this — always fragments. Never the whole thing.

"Said a man who hunts without knowing the way of it..." He wrings the rag. "...gets thinned from the herd." Circles. Circles. "Never said what that meant. Never said what 'the way of it' was. Just: don't hunt unless you KNOW."

"Know what?"

Brennan looks at you. Long look. Then he shakes his head. "That's the thing, isn't it? I don't know. He never told me. I'm not a hunter either." He hangs the rag on its peg. "I just know there's something you're supposed to do when you kill. And if you don't know what it is, you don't kill."

He blows out the last candle. "Good brandy tonight, Halpin. Lock up when you go."

You sit alone in the dark pub and think: *there's a rule. A ritual. Something at the kill. And if you don't know it, you don't hunt. That's why non-hunters don't hunt. Not custom. Not preference. PROTECTION.*

---

### Filberta Again, Another Night

Weeks later. She's sober this time, and quieter. The squirrel is awake, sitting on the bartop eating a hazelnut she brought from the forest.

"When I bring her back to E7," Filberta says, watching the squirrel, "they all come. The whole..." She smiles. "I call them the Parliament. Because they look like little politicians arguing on a branch."

"Parliament?"

"Dozens of them. They cluster around her and just — talk. Chattering. Like they're catching up on news." The squirrel cracks the nut. Shell fragments on the bar. "She goes calm there. Settled. She doesn't do that anywhere else."

You watch the squirrel. Bright-eyed. Alert. Tail flicking.

"They're smart, Halpin. SMART smart. Not animal-smart. They—" She stops herself. Looks sideways at you. "Never mind."

"What?"

"Nothing. Forget I said anything." She scoops up the squirrel. "I should go. Early start tomorrow."

She leaves. You wipe the hazelnut shell off the bar and think: *Parliament. Dozens. They catch up on news. That's not how she described squirrels when she was nine.*

---

### A Young Hunter Whose Name You've Forgotten

He came in shaking. Literally shaking — hands, shoulders, jaw. Dropped onto a stool and said "Brandy" in a voice like gravel. You poured. He drank it in one go. You poured again.

Third cup before he spoke:

"I went off-trail. In E7. Pushed further NE than I'd ever gone." He stared at nothing. "The trees changed. Got wilder. And then I saw... formations. In the ground. Green. Like crystal. Like WAVES."

"Waves?"

"Frozen waves. Malachite green. Banded. Running in a line — curved, like a wall. I could see through them but..." He shook his head. "I didn't cross. Something in me said DON'T. So I didn't."

He finished his drink. Left coin on the bar. "Don't tell anyone I went that far. Dilwen'd skin me."

You've never told anyone. You've never forgotten it either. *Malachite waves. A border. Something that says DON'T.*

## What Helior Tells You

Not in words. Never in words. But in warmth, in direction, in the behaviour of flame and light:

- The glade draws your eye every time you look south-east from the bluff. Not the forest — the OPENING. The place where light gets in.
- Your consecrated flame burns steadier when you face that direction. Not something you've tested. Something you've noticed without meaning to.
- On solstice mornings, the sun's first unobstructed rays shoot out over the forest and drop directly into that glade. For twenty minutes it BLAZES — golden light flooding the lightning-bolt shape while everything else is shadow. You've watched this alone, year after year.

You don't know what's out there. But Helior wants you to go look.
