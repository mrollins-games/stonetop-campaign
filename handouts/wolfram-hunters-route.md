He enters from E5 (the bottom) by swimming across the river from D4. He's never been to D8 or deep into E7 — those are hunter territory and he's a smith. He has NOT discovered the Restless Oak — from E5, her signal is lost in Danu's saturated presence across the whole coppice. ## The Route (what everyone in Stonetop knows)

The hunters go into the Great Wood through a steep hillside pass, down into deep forest where the game is thick, and out to a glade by the river. D8, E7, E5. Three hexes. A day's walk each way if you know the ground.

You are not a hunter. You have never climbed the Rooted Pass or walked through the deep coppice where the trappers work. That country belongs to Dilwen and his kind — the ones who bring you broken snare-anchors and dull skinning knives and say "fix this by morning, would you?"

But you know the other end. You know E5.

---

## The Crossing (D4 → E5)

The river between D4 and E5 runs fast and cold. Chest-high in a good summer, higher in spring. The riverbed is treacherous — smooth stones shift, algae-slick, sudden drop-offs where your footing vanishes. The undertow grabs at your legs in ways that don't match the surface current.

You swim it anyway. You've always swum it. The earth on the other side calls to you — has called since you were old enough to hear it. Danu's warmth is stronger there, in the Wild, than anywhere in the village except directly at the Stone.

> The cold hits your chest. The current shoves. Your feet leave the bottom and for a moment you're nothing but the river's plaything. Then — a nudge. A current that shouldn't exist pushes you toward solid rock. Your hand finds a grip where no grip should be. Every time.

You are not lucky. Someone is helping you.

---

## The Girl in the River

She's there every time you cross. You felt her before you ever saw her — a presence in the water, sad and focused, old beyond reckoning. She doesn't speak. She acts.

The first time you addressed her directly — standing chest-deep mid-crossing, water rushing past — she appeared. A pale shape beneath the surface, looking up. Young face. Dark hair streaming with the current. She mouths words but nothing comes. Her eyes are old and desperate with purpose: *not again, not another one, not here.*

She guides every crossing. Everyone in Stonetop thinks the ford is "surprisingly safe." They've no idea why. You do.

You've tried to help her. You've offered. She doesn't want help. She wants to do her job. She wants no one else to drown where she drowned. That's all that's left of her.

---

## The Welcome Glade (E5)

On the far side of the crossing, the forest opens into a long clearing — shaped like a lightning bolt, widest at the river, narrowing in sharp angles northward into the trees. Grass, wildflowers, light. You feel it before you see it:

> The earth hums. A current beneath your feet — not water, not root, something else. Flowing north and south along the length of the glade. The trees lean away from it on both sides, as if politely making room. The air itself feels lighter.

A spirit lives here. Not IN the glade — the spirit IS the glade. Breathy, eager, faintly nervous. The first time it spoke to you, you almost laughed — it sounded like an excited child showing you a drawing.

*"Yes! Yes, this way — come, I'll show you, come..."*

It wants you to walk. North, into the forest. Or south, toward the river and home. It doesn't care which direction — only that someone is here, moving, using the path it holds open. It's been waiting a very long time for company.

---

## Dilwen's Camp (what you've seen from the south end)

At the narrow northern tip of the glade, where the lightning bolt pinches to a point — a hazel bower. Living poles bent together, oiled hide over the frame. Dilwen's semi-permanent camp. You've seen it from a distance on your way through. The hazel there grows unnaturally bright — vivid yellow-green, vigorous beyond reason. The energy pools there, at the tip, concentrating like water gathering at the lowest point.

Dilwen doesn't know why his camp feels good. He just knows it does.

---

## What the Earth Tells You (looking north from E5)

Standing barefoot in the glade, palms against the soil, Danu's warmth flows through you. The root network extends north — far beyond the glade, deep into the forest the hunters use. You've felt it many times:

> Roots connected underground like a web. Grafted together — different species, one system. Alive, healthy, clean. Nourished by something ancient. The current you stand on continues north, feeding everything it passes through. The system is immense. Old. Slowly fading — like a fire no one tends. But still warm.

You've never followed it north on foot. From here, the coppice reads as one vast, warm presence — Danu, everywhere, filling the space completely. No individual spirits stand out. The goddess saturates the land so thoroughly that Spirit Tongue finds nothing to distinguish. It's warmth within warmth. A candle indistinguishable from sunlight.

You've always assumed that was all there was — Danu's domain, perfectly expressed.

You've never approached from the other direction.

## What the Hunters Tell You (at the forge)

They come to your workshop. They talk while you work:

**About the deep forest (E7):**
"Game's always good there. Boar, deer, fox, marten. Never goes thin." The older hunters say it with a shrug — just how it is. No one questions it. A few have noticed the tree spacing. Most haven't. "Don't overthink it. Be grateful."

Filberta has tried to describe it to you. More than once. But every time, the words go sideways — she ends up talking about her squirrels, about the way the light moves, about how it *feels* to be surrounded by the colony. The place itself escapes her language. You nod. You don't understand. You've never stood in it.

**About E5 (the glade):**
"That's where Dilwen camps. Good ford. Dangerous but manageable." They don't explain why it's manageable. They don't know.

You do.

## Stories

### The first time you heard her (the drowned girl)

You were fifteen. Colyn had told you — again — that he'd found you in the Old Woods as a baby. That the earth had cradled you. That Danu placed you there for him to find. You were angry with him that day — angry at not knowing, angry at being different, angry at the way stones whispered your name when no one else could hear.

You went to the river. You swam across without thinking — the current pushing, the cold biting, and then halfway across: a hand on your back. Firm. Pushing you toward air when the undertow grabbed your legs.

You spun in the water. Nothing. Just the current and the cold and the far bank getting closer.

But you FELT her. Grief and purpose, woven together so tightly they'd become the same thing. You made it across. You stood dripping on the far bank and said, out loud: "Thank you."

The water didn't answer. But something in it settled. Acknowledged.

You've never crossed without greeting her since.

### The trail spirit's loneliness

The trail spirit talks too much. It talks because no one has talked to it in — years? Decades? It doesn't measure time well. It just knows *empty* and *company.*

One evening you sat in the glade's wide mouth by the river, watching the sunset, and the spirit chattered at you for an hour. Not words, exactly — images, feelings, nudges. *Here — sit here, the grass is softest. There — look there, the heron is fishing. Now — now the light is best, see how it falls?*

It was showing you its home. Proudly. Nervously. The way a child shows a parent something they've made.

You stayed until the stars came out. It quieted eventually — not because it ran out of things to show you, but because your presence alone was enough. Someone was there. Someone was walking its path. That's all it ever wanted.
