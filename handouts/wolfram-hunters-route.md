## D4 — The Crossing

You were sixteen. The river between D4 and E5 runs fast and cold — chest-high in a good summer, higher in spring. The riverbed is treacherous: smooth stones shift, algae-slick, sudden drop-offs where your footing vanishes. The undertow grabs at your legs in ways that don't match the surface current.

Townsfolk don't cross here. When someone tries — some wild-headed youth who fancies the far bank — they don't make it. Not that anyone can remember a drowning. They just... come back. Current pushes them to the near shore. Most everyone accepts this.

You tried too. You waded in — the cold hit your chest, the current shoved — and hands you couldn't see pressed against your ribs, firm and insistent, pushing you back toward the shallows. Not violent. Motherly. *No. Not you either. Back.*

You stood waist-deep in the near shallows, breathing hard, and you spoke to her.

> "I can feel you. I know you're there. I need to cross."

Silence. The river rushing past. Then — a shift. The pressure on your chest released. The current didn't change, but it stopped *fighting* you. A presence in the water, old and sad and focused, considered you. Considered your request.

>"Danu calls me from the other side, her trees part there to embrace us."

>Ghost: "Perhase she was who called me too, but I couldn't make it. I called for help but the river filled my lungs, it was a painful deth. One I won't allow again, not with me here."

"Let me finish then what you hoped to start, let me plant there in your name and call it complete."

> The cold hits your chest again. The current shoves. Your feet leave the bottom and for a moment you're nothing but the river's plaything. Then — a nudge. A current that shouldn't exist pushes you toward solid rock. Your hand finds a grip where no grip should be.

She helped you across. She's helped you every time since.

### The Girl in the River

She doesn't speak much. She acts. The first time you addressed her from the far bank — standing dripping on E5's shore, looking back — she appeared. A pale shape beneath the surface, looking up. Young face. Dark hair streaming with the current. She mouths words but nothing comes. Her eyes are old and desperate with purpose: *not again, not another one, not here.*

She guides every crossing. She redirects everyone else considering the other side folly. Swimmers feel the current bringing them back to the near shore — safer. They think the ford is impassable. It isn't. She just won't let them drown and guides them to the greatest safety, back with kin.

You've tried to help her. You've offered. She doesn't want help. She wants to do her job. She wants no one else to drown where she drowned. That's all that's left of her.

**What you know:**
- No one in Stonetop knows she exists. They just think the crossing is impossible.
- She drowned here — a long time ago. Longer than anyone remembers.
- She doesn't distinguish between "dangerous crosser" and "safe crosser" — she turns EVERYONE back. Except you. Because together, redemption.
- She's a ghost, not a nature spirit. Mortal dead.
- You've never crossed without greeting her since that first day.

## E5 — The Welcome Glade

On the far side of the crossing, the forest opens. That first day, dripping river water, you stepped into sunlight and stopped.

A long clearing — shaped like a lightning bolt, widest at the river, narrowing in sharp angles northward into the trees. Grass, wildflowers, light. You felt it before you understood it:

> The earth hums. A current beneath your feet — not water, not root, something else. Flowing north and south along the length of the glade. The trees lean away from it on both sides, as if politely making room. The air itself feels lighter.

And then something moved.

A rabbit — brown, common, sitting in the grass thirty feet ahead. Watching you. Ears up. Not afraid. You took a step forward and it bolted — not away from you, but *along the glade*, north, fast, then stopped and looked back. Waiting.

You took another step. It ran again. Stopped. Looked back.

*"Yes! Yes, this way — come, I'll show you, come..."*

The voice wasn't in your ears. It was in the soles of your feet, in the hum of the earth, in the eager twitching of the rabbit's nose. The rabbit wasn't the spirit — the spirit was the glade, and the rabbit was just how it chose to greet you. An invitation. A race.

You ran. You couldn't help it. The rabbit sprinted north along the lightning-bolt path and you chased it, laughing, bare feet in the grass, the earth singing beneath you. It zigged where the glade zigged. It zagged where the glade zagged. At the narrow northern tip, where the trees closed in overhead, the rabbit vanished — and in its place, a figure stood on the path ahead. A courier, lean and road-worn, who tipped an invisible hat and dissolved into dappled light.

The spirit wants travelers to travel. It wants someone on its path. It wants to race.

**What you know:**
- The glade IS the spirit and the spirit IS the glade — tethered here, unable to leave.
- It presents as a rabbit sometimes. A courier other times. Always something that moves, something that goes.
- It loves when someone agrees to race. Doesn't care about direction — north into the forest or south toward the river. Just movement. Just company.
- It's been waiting a very long time for someone who can hear it.
- You've come back many times. You always race it. It always wins.

### Dilwen's Camp

At the narrow northern tip of the glade, where the lightning bolt pinches to a point — a hazel bower. Living poles bent together, oiled hide over the frame. Dilwen's semi-permanent camp. You saw it that first day, empty. The hazel grows unnaturally bright — vivid yellow-green, vigorous beyond reason. Energy pools here, at the tip, concentrating like water gathering at the lowest point.

On later visits, you've sometimes seen Filberta here — waiting for her father, playing with her squirrel, looking bored and restless the way she always does when Dilwen leaves her behind.

**What you since learned:**
- Dilwen doesn't know why his camp is so but here he feels special, no one but he and his crew have the privilage of making camp here - and it will stay that way.

## E7 — The Coppice

That first day, you didn't stop at the glade. The rabbit-spirit had pulled you north, and at the glade's tip the energy in the earth didn't end — it came from the north.

Filberta was ahead of you on the trail.

You'd seen her from behind — small figure, squirrel on her shoulder, moving quickly through the undergrowth with the confidence of someone who'd walked this path a hundred times. She didn't see you. You didn't call out. You just... followed. The energy beneath your feet was a compass needle, pointing the same direction she walked.

The forest changed.

> The undergrowth thinned. Massive trunks rose in patterns that felt deliberate. Oaks, beeches, hazel — growing in layers, spaced too evenly. The ground carpeted in acorn caps, beech husks, hazelnut shells. Game trails converging from every direction. And beneath it all — beneath your feet, in the soil, in the roots — Danu. Everywhere. Not a voice, not a presence you could name. The goddess herself, filling the space so completely that nothing else stood out.

You stopped walking. You knelt. You pressed your palms flat against the earth.

> Roots connected underground like a web. Grafted together — different species, one system. Alive, healthy, clean. Nourished by the same current you'd followed from the glade. The system was immense. Old. Slowly fading — like a fire no one tends. But still warm. Still breathing.

Spirit Tongue found. Danu saturated the land so thoroughly that no lesser spirit could manifest — not because they were absent, but because the goddess was already *everything here.*

Filberta disappeared into the trees ahead. You stayed. You didn't need to go further. You'd found something vast — something that answered questions you hadn't known to ask. The earth held you. Danu's palm, open and warm.

You turned back before dark. You've returned many times since — following the same path, kneeling in the same place, feeling the same slow-fading warmth. But you've never ventured past E7 into the highlands beyond. Why would you? Everything you needed was already here.

**What you know:**
- The root network is immense and grafted — different species connected underground into one system. This is intentional. Someone did this.
- Danu's presence here is total. No individual spirits manifest because the goddess fills the space completely. Spirit Tongue finds nothing to distinguish.
- The system is slowly weakening. Fading. Like it needs tending that no one provides.
- You've seen Filberta here many times — she belongs to this place in a way the other hunters don't. The squirrels come to her. The forest knows her.
- Hunters don't tell non-hunters about the coppice. You've never been told about it — you found it yourself. What little you know of hunter custom says this place is theirs, and silence is expected.
- You've never approached from the D8 side. Never had reason to — the river crossing is YOUR way in, and from E5 north is all you've ever needed.
